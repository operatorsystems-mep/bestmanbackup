Best man rings: how to keep and hand over the wedding rings

Collect, pocket, check twice, hand over. The rings job, step by step.

Updated 17 November 2026

THE SHORT ANSWER

Confirm early whether you are holding the rings. Collect them on the morning from whoever has them, keep both in a secure inside jacket pocket or a small pouch, check them before you leave and before you walk in, then step forward when the celebrant or registrar asks and hand them over. Agree the exact cue at the rehearsal.

Is the best man always in charge of the rings?

Often, but not always. The Church of England notes that looking after the rings is a traditional best man job. Some couples give the rings to a ring bearer for the walk, then the best man takes over. Some leave them with the celebrant. Ask the couple. Do not assume.

Put the answer in writing in your day plan, next to the ceremony time. Our wedding day guide and duties checklist cover the wider job.

When to collect the rings

  • Night before: Confirm who has them and where they sleep. Do not leave this to a morning scramble.
  • Morning of: Collect both rings once the groom is dressed, or earlier if that is safer. Count them aloud.
  • Before you leave: Pocket check. Phone, wallet, cue cards, rings.
  • At the venue: One more quiet check before you walk down or take your place.

If fees, licences or envelopes travel with you as well, keep them in a different pocket so a ring hunt does not tip paperwork onto the floor.

Where to keep them

Use an inside jacket pocket with a secure lining, or a small zip pouch that fits that pocket. Trouser pockets are a common way rings go walking. Do not leave them on a table while photos happen. Do not hand them to a usher “just for a minute” without a clear hand-back.

  • Both rings together in one place.
  • Nothing sharp or loose in the same pocket.
  • If the jacket comes off for photos, the rings stay on your person or go to a named backup for that window only.

How the hand-over works

At a Church of England service, the best man typically steps forward and hands the rings to the vicar when asked, often to be placed on a book before the exchange. Civil ceremonies and celebrants vary. The rehearsal is where you learn the cue: a look, a line, or a pause.

  1. Stand where you were told to stand.
  2. When asked, step forward calmly. Do not dig for thirty seconds.
  3. Hand both rings over the way you practised — to the celebrant, or one then the other if that is the plan.
  4. Step back beside the groom.

If a child is the ring bearer, agree who takes the rings from the pillow or box, and when. An adult should supervise the transfer.

Backup plan if something goes wrong

  • Empty pocket panic: Stay still. Check the pouch, the other inside pocket, then quietly ask the groom or an usher you briefed. Do not announce it to the room.
  • Only one ring: Proceed with what you have if the couple and celebrant agree; many couples still complete the vows and sort the second ring after.
  • You are not holding them: Know who is. Your job becomes making sure that person is in place on time.

None of this needs inventing drama. A calm best man is the plan.

UK and US notes

In UK church and civil ceremonies the hand-over is usually to the officiant. In many US ceremonies the best man still holds the rings, sometimes with the maid of honour holding one. Confirm at the rehearsal dinner or rehearsal itself. See also best man vs groomsmen vs ushers.

Frequently asked questions

Does the best man hold both wedding rings?

Often yes, but check. Some couples split the rings between the best man and maid of honour, or use a ring bearer for the entrance only.

When does the best man give the rings over?

When the celebrant, registrar or vicar asks during the ceremony. Learn the exact cue at the rehearsal.

Where should the best man keep the rings?

In a secure inside jacket pocket or a small zip pouch in that pocket. Avoid loose trouser pockets.

What if there is a ring bearer?

Agree who takes the rings from the bearer and when. An adult should supervise so the rings end up with the right person before the vows.

Should I practise with the real rings?

Practise the movement at the rehearsal. Keep the real rings secure; you do not need to juggle them all morning.
