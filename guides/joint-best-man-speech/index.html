TWO BEST MEN, ONE SPEECH

How to write a joint best man speech

THE SHORT ANSWER

A joint best man speech works when you plan it as one speech with two voices, not two speeches back to back. Agree the total length (about seven to ten minutes combined, not double a single speech), split the material by theme or by era, script the handovers, and rehearse together at least twice so the back-and-forth feels natural.

Why couples choose joint best men

More grooms now have two best men: a brother and a best friend, two friends from different stages of life, or two people who simply couldn't be split. It's a lovely way to honour both relationships, and it halves the pressure on each of you. It does mean the speech needs more planning.

Options for structuring a joint speech

ApproachHow it worksBest for
Split by eraOne covers childhood or school, the other adult lifeA brother and a friend, or friends from different times
Split by themeEach takes a different trait or side of the groomFriends from the same group
Back-and-forthShort alternating sections, sometimes finishing each other's linesConfident pairs who rehearse well
One leads, one addsOne gives most of the speech, the other tells one story and gives the toastWhen one of you is nervous or less comfortable speaking

Pick the approach that suits your personalities. A double act needs real rehearsal. A clean split by era is simpler and still works well.

Step by step

1. Meet early and agree the basics

Before writing anything, agree:

  • Total length. Aim for about seven to ten minutes combined, roughly 900 to 1,300 words at around 130 words a minute. Speechwriting guides suggest six to eight or seven to eight minutes, and all of them warn against doubling the length. See how long a best man speech should be.
  • Tone. If one of you is naturally funny and the other is sentimental, use it, but agree the overall feel.
  • Who opens and who toasts. Often whoever opens doesn't toast, so both of you get a big moment.
  • What's off limits. Agree it together with the couple so nobody gets surprised.

2. Pool your stories

Each of you writes a list of stories and traits. Put them side by side. You'll find overlap, which tells you what the groom is really known for, and you'll find gaps each of you can fill.

3. Divide the material

Assign each story to one person. Agree a single theme so your halves point the same way. If one of you covers his reliability with a childhood story and the other with an adult one, the speech feels joined up.

4. Write separately, then stitch together

Write your parts on your own, then put them in one document and read it through together. Cut repeats, smooth the transitions and check the total length.

5. Script the handovers

Handovers are where joint speeches wobble. Write them word for word.

Dan: "...and that was the last time Tom was allowed near a barbecue. But for what happened when he moved to London, I'll hand over to someone who had to live with him."

Raj: "Thanks, Dan. Yes, I was his flatmate for three years. I've had therapy since."

6. Rehearse together, in person

At least twice, ideally standing up, with cue cards. Agree where you'll stand, who holds the microphone, and how you pass it. Practise the toast together so you don't both start it.

Who does what: a sample split

  1. Best man A: opening line, introduces both of you, quick thank-you if requested.
  2. Best man A: story from school or childhood.
  3. Handover.
  4. Best man B: story from adult life.
  5. Best man B: how the groom changed when he met his partner.
  6. Both: a short line each about the couple.
  7. Best man A or B: the toast.

Opening lines for a joint speech

  • "We're doing this together, partly so we can share the stories, and mostly so we can share the blame."
  • "Tom couldn't choose between us. We've taken that as a compliment, though we suspect he just couldn't make a decision."
  • "Between us, we've known Tom for forty years. He's only been nice for about six of them."

Common joint-speech mistakes

  • Two full speeches. Two back-to-back solo speeches easily pass the ten-minute ceiling most experts give.
  • Telling the same story twice. Swap drafts early.
  • One person dominating. Not always a problem if agreed, but if it's meant to be equal, check timings.
  • Long in-jokes between you. The banter between best men only works if the room's in on it.
  • No plan for the microphone. Decide who holds it and when it moves.
  • Starting the toast together. Decide who says "please raise your glasses".

When the two best men don't know each other

A brother and an old schoolfriend may barely have met. Get together, or at least have a long call, well before the wedding. Telling each other stories about the groom is the best icebreaker, and it'll make your handovers warmer on the day. If you're in different cities, a shared document plus two video rehearsals works.

Best man and best woman, or other combinations

The same principles apply to a best man and best woman, a best man and a "best person", or any pair. Use whatever titles the couple prefer and introduce each other clearly so guests know who you are to the groom.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a joint best man speech be?

About seven to ten minutes combined, roughly 900 to 1,300 words, rather than two full speeches back to back.

How do you split a joint best man speech?

Split by era (childhood and adult life), by theme, or by having one person lead and the other add a story and the toast. Agree one theme so both halves point the same way.

Who gives the toast in a joint best man speech?

Either can, but agree it in advance. Often the person who didn't open gives the toast, so both of you get a big moment.

How do you hand over in a joint best man speech?

Script the handover word for word, usually with a line that sets up the other person's story, and agree who holds the microphone.

Can a groom have more than one best man?

Yes. Traditionally there's one, but Hitched notes there's no strict rule, and many grooms choose two to honour a brother and a best friend equally.
