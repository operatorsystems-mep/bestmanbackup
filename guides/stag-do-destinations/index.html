THE STAG DO

Stag do destinations: UK and Europe without the guesswork

Updated 18 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Pick the destination after you know the budget, the groom’s hard noes, and who can travel. UK cities keep cost and logistics simpler; Europe works when flights and passports are realistic for everyone. Use public operator rankings as a shortlist, then build your own numbers before you book.

Decide the filters before the city

  • Per-head budget including travel — see stag do budget and cheap stag do ideas.
  • Passports, time off, and the earliest anyone can leave on Friday.
  • Nightlife vs activity vs quiet — ask the groom, not the loudest mate.
  • Whether drink is central or optional — non-drinking ideas.

Full planning order: how to plan a stag do.

UK destinations groups actually book

UK stag operators that publish destination rankings — including Funktion Events’ long-running location lists — repeatedly surface Newcastle, Bristol, Cardiff, Edinburgh, Liverpool and London for domestic weekends. Treat rankings as demand signals from their bookings, not as a quality score for your friendship group.

  • Newcastle / Liverpool / Bristol: walkable nightlife, strong day activities, no passport risk.
  • Edinburgh / Cardiff: denser pub culture; good for mixed ages.
  • London: maximum choice, higher spend — set the number first.

Europe: value, sun and short flights

Public 2026 operator surveys (for example LastFiesta’s destination and price barometer pieces) commonly place Krakow, Prague and Budapest at the lower end for a two-night city spend excluding flights, with Lisbon and Barcelona mid-range, and Amsterdam higher. Their published per-person city totals are operator field estimates — always rebuild your own spreadsheet with live flights and rooms.

  • Value nightlife: Prague, Krakow, Budapest.
  • Sun and coast: Barcelona, Lisbon; Spanish beach resorts appear often in UK operator top tens.
  • Short hop, premium spend: Amsterdam, Dublin.

How to shortlist in one evening

  1. Write three cities that fit the budget filter.
  2. Price the same weekend: outbound, return, one apartment, one headline activity.
  3. Check stag or group house rules on the listing.
  4. Poll the group with numbers attached, not vibes alone.
  5. Book rooms only for confirmed payers.

Build a fair comparison sheet

City marketing will always sound fun. Your spreadsheet should not. For each shortlisted destination, price the same skeleton weekend:

  • Outbound and return for the actual dates
  • One shared apartment or hotel for the confirmed headcount
  • One headline activity with a realistic per-person fee
  • Two group meals
  • A buffer line for taxis and the things nobody remembered
  • The groom’s share, divided across the paying group

Then add soft factors: walkability, how late the airport runs, whether anyone needs accessible rooms, and whether the groom hates big clubs. A “cheap” city that forces three taxis an hour is not cheap.

Passports, insurance and house rules

Check passport validity early for Europe. Tell the group in writing. Travel insurance is dull and useful when someone misses a connection or needs a clinic. On accommodation, message hosts about stag or hen groups before you pay — some listings ban them outright. Paying deposits into a listing that will cancel on arrival is an expensive way to learn to read the rules.

Sample weekend shapes

UK city: arrive Friday evening, one activity Saturday afternoon, reserved dinner, games in the apartment, gentle Sunday exit. Europe value city: Friday night arrival, Saturday activity plus walkable nightlife, Sunday lunch and flight. Sun destination: build shade, water and a backup indoor plan into the day, not only a beach idea that dies in a heatwave.

Whatever the city, appoint a co-pilot so you can also be present. Destination energy plus sole admin burns best men out before the wedding week starts.

Reading operator rankings sensibly

Funktion Events, StagWeb and similar companies publish destination popularity from their own bookings. That tells you what groups buy through them. It does not tell you that Newcastle is “better” than Bath for your quiet groom who hates clubs. Use rankings to build a longlist, then filter with his personality and your spreadsheet.

Price barometers that quote per-person city totals excluding flights are helpful for shape, not for checkout. Beer prices move. Apartment prices spike on bank holidays. Always re-price the exact weekend.

When the group is split across budgets

Offer a core destination everyone can afford and an optional extra night for those who want it. Do not let the highest earners silently set a city that excludes the people the groom actually wants there. If two camps form, show the numbers once and let the groom break the tie.

Activity-first versus nightlife-first

Some groups want a morning of go-karts and an early night. Others want a single club as the point of the weekend. Match the city to that preference. Prague and Budapest reward walkable bar crawls; coastal Spanish resorts reward day-to-night beach club rhythms; UK activity cities like Bristol reward daytime bookings followed by harbourside pubs.

Book the headline activity before you brag about the nightlife. Activities sell out; bars rarely do. If the activity is the memory the groom will keep, protect it in the budget even if it means fewer rounds.

Exit plans matter

Know the last train, the airport transfer time, and who holds boarding passes. A destination that is perfect on Saturday and miserable at 4 a.m. Sunday with a missed flight is a failure of planning, not of vibe. Put the return logistics in the same note as the bar list.

Budget bands without fake numbers

Skip invented averages. Ask each person for a hard ceiling that includes travel, bed, activities and a contribution to the groom’s share. Rank destinations by that ceiling, not by Instagram. A UK activity weekend with a shared house often fits a tighter band than a European capital with flights and city-centre hotels.

Publish the band before you book anything non-refundable. People who cannot stretch need an honest off-ramp early, not a guilt trip after the deposit.

When to choose staycation over abroad

Choose the UK when the group has mixed passport situations, short leave, or a wedding the following weekend. Choose abroad when the groom has a place he has always wanted and the group can afford the transfer time. Neither option is more “proper” — the right one is the one everyone can actually attend.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best stag do destination in the UK?

There is no single best city. Newcastle, Bristol, Liverpool, Cardiff and Edinburgh appear often in operator booking lists because they balance nightlife and logistics.

What is a good cheap European stag destination?

Operator price surveys often point to Krakow, Prague and Budapest for lower city costs. Always add your own flight prices.

Should we go abroad?

Only if passports, budgets and time off work for the whole invited list. A strong UK weekend beats a stressful flight.

When should we book?

As soon as deposits are in and the date is fixed. Rooms and group activities disappear first on popular weekends.

How do I stop the group arguing?

Show three priced options and vote once. Re-open only if the winner becomes impossible to book.
