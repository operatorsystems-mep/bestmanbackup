DUTIES AND THE WEDDING DAY

Best man outfit: what to wear and what to check

Updated 18 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Wear what the couple ask you to wear. Usually that means matching the groom’s party with a small difference so he still reads as the groom. Confirm dress code early, book hire or alterations six to eight weeks out, do a full try-on with shoes, and pack a tiny repair kit for the day.

Start with the dress code, not Pinterest

Ask the couple four things: formal level, colour, hire or buy, and who is paying. UK tradition often has the couple covering matching hire for the groom’s side; US groomsmen more often pay themselves. Settle it before anyone books — details in what the best man pays for.

Common UK looks

LookWhen it fitsNotes
Morning suitFormal daytime church or country houseTailcoat, waistcoat, striped trousers; top hat often carried
Lounge / three-piece suitMost modern weddingsNavy, charcoal or grey remain the safe defaults
Black tie / dinner suitEvening formalBow tie, proper shoes; follow the invitation
Lighter fabricsSummer outdoorLinen and cotton blends crease — plan for it

Bridebook’s morning-suit guide describes typical UK hire in a published range of about £100–£250 depending on retailer and what is included; re-check current prices before you budget. John Lewis and specialist hire shops also publish rental ranges that move with season and brand.

How to match the groom without competing

  • Same silhouette and colour family as the groomsmen.
  • Let the groom take the distinct waistcoat, tie or boutonnière if that is the plan.
  • Shoes and belt in the same tone across the party.
  • Check pocket squares and cufflinks so nobody invents a solo theme.

Your job includes herding fittings for ushers if asked — see best man vs groomsmen vs ushers.

Timeline for hire and fittings

  • 6–8 weeks out: book the shop; get measured; order.
  • 2–3 weeks out: try the full outfit with the shoes you will wear.
  • Week of: confirm collection time; press or steam; check buttons.
  • Day after: return hire on time; note damage before you leave the counter.

Day-of outfit checklist

  • Jacket, trousers, waistcoat, shirt, tie or cravat
  • Shoes cleaned; spare laces if you are unlucky
  • Cufflinks, boutonnière pin, lint roller
  • Fashion tape, safety pins, stain wipe, pain relief
  • Inside pocket clear for rings pouch and cue cards

Fit details that show in photos

Photographers stand closer than you think. Jacket shoulders should sit clean; sleeve length should show a little shirt cuff; trouser break should be neat, not puddled. If the hire shop offers a quick alteration, take it. A perfect colour in a bad size still looks wrong in the album.

  • Sit down in the trousers at the fitting. Wedding days involve chairs.
  • Raise your arms as if celebrating. The jacket should not lift like a cape.
  • Check the waistcoat does not gape when you sit.
  • Wear the exact shirt and shoes you will wear on the day for the final try-on.

Weather, venues and backups

Outdoor lawns and cobbled courtyards punish leather soles and pale suits. Ask about terrain. Pack a small brush or wipe for dust. If rain is likely, know where coats live and who holds umbrellas for photos — sometimes that is you.

Buttonholes wilt. Know who supplies them and when they are pinned. Carry a spare pin. If anyone in the party has never worn a tie, do a practice knot the night before rather than inventing one on camera.

Cultural and dress-code edge cases

Some ceremonies ask for morning dress; some ask for national or cultural dress alongside Western suits; some are deliberately casual. The invitation and the couple beat any blog. If you are unsure whether trainers are a charming choice or a problem, ask. Do not be the only person who “interpreted” the code creatively.

For black-tie evening weddings, follow black-tie rules unless the couple say otherwise. Novelty ties that photograph as a joke in the group chat often photograph as a distraction in formal portraits.

Accessories that quietly fail

Black shoes with a brown belt, a crooked boutonnière, a tie knot that sits off-centre — these are the details guests cannot name and photographers cannot hide. Assign five minutes on the morning for a mutual check: you check the groom, an usher checks you.

  • Pocket square: tidy, not a sail.
  • Socks: long enough that bare leg does not appear when you sit on stage.
  • Watch and cufflinks: decide one metallic tone.
  • Phone bulge: move the brick to a bag or inside jacket while photos happen.

Hire-day logistics

Know the return deadline before the wedding, not after. If you are travelling to a destination wedding, ask whether the hire shop can ship or whether buying is saner. Label garment bags with names when multiple suits travel in one car. Steam at the hotel if the venue has no press — phone the hotel ahead.

If a groomsman’s suit fails to arrive, your job is calm Plan B: local hire, a dark backup jacket, or a retailer that can do emergency alterations. Panic helps nobody. A written list of shop numbers in your day sheet helps a lot.

US notes: tuxes and destination weddings

US weddings more often mean tuxedo hire paid by each groomsman. Confirm colour of tie, cummerbund or waistcoat, and whether black tie is required after a certain hour. Destination weddings add packing weight and humidity — choose fabrics you can travel with and steam on arrival. If the ceremony is on sand, ask about shoe plans before you buy new leather soles.

Whatever the country, take one full-length mirror photo of yourself in the finished outfit a week out. You will spot problems cameras catch and bathroom mirrors miss.

Frequently asked questions

Does the best man have to match the groom?

Usually you match the groom’s party. The groom may wear a small difference so he still stands out. Follow what the couple decide.

Should I hire or buy?

Hire if you will not wear it again. Buy if you will use the suit and the budget works.

When should I get fitted?

About six to eight weeks before is a common hire timeline, with a final try-on closer to the day.

Who pays for the best man’s suit in the UK?

Traditionally the couple often cover matching hire, but check. US practice more often has groomsmen paying themselves.

What shoes should I wear?

Clean, formal shoes that match the belt and the rest of the party. Break them in before the day.
