SAFE HUMOUR

Best man speech jokes that are safe for a mixed room

THE SHORT ANSWER

The jokes that work in a best man speech are specific, affectionate and understandable by everyone in the room, from the groom's gran to the partner's colleagues. Build humour from real stories about the groom's habits rather than borrowed one-liners, test every joke against a simple "would his gran and his new in-laws laugh?" check, and cut anything about exes, sex or the partner's family.

What makes a best man joke work

The best laughs at weddings rarely come from jokes in the stand-up sense. They come from recognition. When you describe something the groom really does, and half the room knows it's true, they laugh because they've seen it. That's why a specific detail ("he irons his jeans") beats any line you'll find in a list of one-liners.

Good wedding humour has three qualities:

  • It's affectionate. You're teasing a friend you clearly love, not scoring points.
  • It's inclusive. Most of the room can follow it without a back-story.
  • It's true, or close enough. Exaggeration is fine. Invention usually falls flat.

The gran test, and four more checks

Before any joke goes into the speech, run it past these five questions. If it fails one, rewrite it or cut it.

  1. Would his gran laugh, or at least not wince? The room includes children, grandparents and people who've just met him.
  2. Would the partner's family be comfortable? They're judging their new son- or daughter-in-law partly through you.
  3. Can most people follow it without context? If you need to explain who "Gaz from Swindon" is, it's an in-joke.
  4. Does it make the groom look like someone worth marrying? A joke can be at his expense, but the overall picture should be a good man.
  5. Would the couple be happy to see it in the wedding video? Because it will be in the wedding video.

Etiquette guides say the same. Debrett's says best man humour should be "never rude, uncomfortable or smutty", Hitched says no swearing or vulgar language, and Toastmasters warns that a toast "isn't the time to roast, embarrass, or overshare".

Types of jokes that land

The habit

Every groom has one. The way he packs, the number of alarms he sets, his relationship with a particular football team. Describe it precisely and exaggerate slightly.

"Tom doesn't just support his team, he suffers for them. He's the only man I know who wears a lucky shirt to the dentist."

The self-deprecating line

Laughing at yourself early tells the room you're not there to humiliate anyone, which buys you goodwill for teasing the groom later.

"Tom says I'm his best man because I'm the most reliable person he knows. Which says a lot about the people he knows."

The callback

Mention something early in the speech, then bring it back later with a twist. Callbacks feel clever and reward people for listening.

Early: "Tom has never once been on time." Later, before the toast: "Jess, I've known him twenty years and he's never been on time for anything. Except, it turns out, for meeting you."

The rule of three

List two normal things, then a third that breaks the pattern.

"Tom's three great loves: his family, his friends, and a 2009 Ford Focus he still talks about like a lost relative."

The turn

A joke that sets up a sincere point. These are the most useful lines in the whole speech, because they carry you from funny to heartfelt.

"Tom is the most stubborn man I know. He's argued with satnavs, referees and his own mother. But that same stubbornness is why he's never once given up on a friend."

Jokes to leave out

  • Exes. Not a name, not a hint, not "and we all remember..."
  • Sex and the wedding night. Guaranteed groans from half the room.
  • Stag do stories. Most guests weren't there, and what happens on the stag often needs to stay there.
  • The partner's looks, weight or family. The partner isn't a target.
  • "She's punching" jokes. Calling the partner too good for him is overused and a bit unkind to both.
  • Mother-in-law jokes. Dated, and the mother-in-law is sitting right there.
  • Anything about money, health, religion or politics. You don't know who's sensitive about what.
  • Drinking as a punchline. One mention is fine. A whole speech about how drunk he gets isn't.

Borrowed jokes and one-liners

You'll find endless lists of best man one-liners online. A professional wedding speechwriter interviewed by BBC Radio 4 specifically warns against clichés and generic gags. A few can work as a framing line, but rely on them and the speech feels generic, and someone at the wedding will have heard the same line at another wedding. If you do use a borrowed line, adapt it with a real detail about the groom so it becomes yours.

How many jokes is enough?

There's no quota. A good rhythm for a five- to seven-minute speech is a laugh in the first fifteen seconds, then roughly one every thirty seconds or so through the stories, then none at all in the final section. The closing lines should be sincere. Our ending guide explains why.

If you're not naturally funny, don't force it. A warm speech with two light moments will go down better than a nervous attempt at stand-up. Our best man speech examples include a heartfelt version.

Delivering a joke

  • Pause before the punchline. Half a second is enough.
  • Wait for the laugh. Wedding speechwriter Heidi Ellert-McDermott told Brides you should never talk over laughter once it lands.
  • If it doesn't land, keep going. Don't explain it or apologise. Nobody minds a joke that misses; everyone minds a best man who panics about it.
  • Look at the groom when you tease him. It shows the affection behind the line.
  • Don't laugh at your own joke first. Smile, by all means.

Testing your jokes before the day

Read the speech aloud to one honest person who knows the groom. Watch them, don't just ask. A real laugh, a smile and a polite nod tell you very different things. Cut anything that got the polite nod. If you have a line you're unsure about, ask the groom's sibling or a close mutual friend rather than the groom himself, so the surprise survives.

Frequently asked questions

What jokes should you not tell in a best man speech?

Leave out exes, sex, stag do stories, jokes about the partner's looks or family, mother-in-law jokes and anything about money, health, religion or politics.

How many jokes should a best man speech have?

There's no set number. A laugh early on, then light moments roughly every half a minute through the stories, works well. Keep the final section sincere.

What if my jokes don't get a laugh?

Carry on without explaining or apologising. A missed joke is quickly forgotten if you stay calm and move to the next line.

Is it OK to roast the groom in a best man speech?

Gentle teasing is expected, but it should be affectionate and leave the groom looking like someone worth marrying. A wedding speech isn't a roast.

Where can I find funny best man stories?

Ask his siblings, parents, old friends and colleagues, and look for his everyday habits. A specific, true detail is usually funnier than any borrowed one-liner.
