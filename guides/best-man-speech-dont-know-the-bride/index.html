WRITING THE SPEECH

Best man speech when you don't know the bride

Updated 18 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

You do not need a fake biography of someone you have met twice. Centre the groom with one true story, add what you have actually seen of them together, welcome her by name without inventing intimacy, then toast the couple. Ask the groom (and, carefully, her) for one safe detail before you write.

This is more common than people admit

Plenty of best men know the groom for years and the bride for months — or for two dinners and a Zoom call. Distance, busy jobs and a short engagement all produce the same gap. Guests do not need you to pretend otherwise. They need you to sound like his friend and like someone who is glad she is here.

UK wedding speech advice (Debrett's-style order notes and widely shared best-man checklists) still expects a word on the couple and a toast to both. That word can be short and honest. It does not have to be a character study.

If you already know her well, you will still find useful structure in how to write a best man speech. This page is for the version where the blank page is her, not him.

What the room actually needs from you

Your job as best man is still to show who he is, keep the room kind, and raise a glass to the marriage. Knowing the bride less well changes only the couple section, not the whole speech.

  • Prove the friendship with one specific story about him.
  • Name her early enough that she is not an afterthought in the last ten seconds.
  • Say one true thing about them together, or about how he is around her.
  • Welcome her without claiming a private bond you do not have.
  • Toast both by name.

Length stays ordinary: three to five minutes for most rooms, five to seven if you are the main UK speaker. See how long a best man speech should be. Going long to fill the gap with vague praise is worse than staying short.

What to ask before you write

Do not invent. Collect.

  • Ask the groom: one thing he loves that a mate is allowed to say in public; any topic that is off-limits; how she prefers to be named in the toast (full name, first name, nickname).
  • Ask a mutual friend who has seen them more than you have for one small, kind detail — not a roast.
  • Ask her only if it feels natural: a short message is enough. “Is there anything you would rather I did not mention?” protects everyone.

Write the answers down. If you cannot get a single usable detail, lean on what you have watched: how he talks about her when she is not there, a plan that includes both of them, a calm you have noticed since they got together. That is enough for a sincere paragraph.

A simple structure that still includes her

Use the same spine as any best man speech. Keep the bride section tight.

  1. Open: your name, how you know him, one light line.
  2. Thanks if the couple want them — keep it brief.
  3. One story about him that shows a trait (loyalty, humour, stubborn kindness).
  4. The couple: her name, what you have seen, a welcome.
  5. Toast to both.

Lines you can adapt (without fake intimacy)

  • “I’ve known Tom for twelve years. I’ve known Emma for closer to twelve dinners — and every one of them made him better company on the way home.”
  • “I won’t pretend I can tell Emma’s life story. I can tell you what I’ve seen: he is steadier, kinder, and somehow on time.”
  • “Emma, I hope you’ll forgive a late arrival to the friendship. On behalf of the slightly chaotic side of Tom’s life, welcome — we’re glad you’re here.”
  • “The brief was simple: don’t embarrass him, do toast you both. Emma, you’ve already made the first half easier.”

Swap names. Cut anything you cannot say with a straight face. If a line needs a private joke to land, it is not ready for her relatives.

What to avoid

  • A long invented portrait of hobbies, childhood and “how perfect they are” when you have not seen it.
  • Comparing her to exes, or joking that you “vetted” her.
  • Stag-do material as proof you know him better than she does.
  • Leaving her unnamed until the toast.
  • Asking the room to fill in who she is (“I’m sure you all know how amazing she is”) when you have said nothing specific.

UK and US notes

In the UK you are often the main comic-and-warm speaker at the end of the breakfast. Guests expect a toast to the couple, so her name belongs in that closing even if your stories are mostly about him.

In the US (best man “toast”), speeches run shorter. The same rule applies harder: one groom story, one honest couple line, toast. Do not spend half the toast apologising for not knowing her — one frank sentence is plenty, then move on.

A worked mini-example

Use this as a shape, not a script to read word for word.

I’m James, Tom’s best man. We met in first year when he borrowed a charger and somehow never gave it back — which, looking back, was the start of a very one-sided lending library.

The thing about Tom is he shows up. When my dad was in hospital he drove across town with terrible coffee and sat there without needing a speech of his own. That is who he is when nobody is posting about it.

I’ve not known Emma as long as some of you. What I have seen is this: he is lighter around her, and she laughs at his worst jokes, which is either love or excellent manners. Emma — welcome. We’re better for having you.

Please raise a glass to Tom and Emma.

That is under two minutes if spoken calmly. Add a second short groom story only if you still have time on the brief.

Frequently asked questions

Is it OK to say I don't know the bride well?

Yes — once, briefly, then say what you have actually seen. Do not apologise for a full minute.

Should I ask her to approve the speech?

You do not need a full approval loop. A quick check on off-limits topics is wise. Let the groom see the draft if that is their preference.

What if I have only met her once?

Lean on how he talks about her and one welcome line. Skip biography.

Can the speech still be funny?

Yes. Keep the jokes on you or on him, not on her absence from your life story.

How do I toast them properly?

Stand, name both, keep the final line simple. See the best man toast guide for short closers.
