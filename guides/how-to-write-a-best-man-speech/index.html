WRITING THE SPEECH

How to write a best man speech, step by step

THE SHORT ANSWER

Collect stories first, choose one that shows who the groom really is, then build around it: a short opening, the story, what it says about him, a word about the couple, and a toast. Write it out in full, cut it to three to five minutes, turn it into cue cards and rehearse it out loud. Give yourself at least a month.

Step 1: start early

A speech written the night before usually sounds like it. Start collecting ideas as soon as you are asked, aim to have a full draft about a month before the wedding, and use the last few weeks for cutting and rehearsing. Speaking a speech out loud a few times does more for your nerves than any last-minute rewrite.

Step 2: collect material

Do not try to write sentences yet. Just gather raw notes:

  • Ten memories with the groom, from any point in your friendship.
  • Three words that describe him, and a moment that proves each one.
  • How he changed after meeting his partner, and anything you have noticed about them together.
  • Stories from other people. Ask his family, old friends and colleagues for one memory each. The stag do is a good time to ask, as long as you write the answers down.

Step 3: choose your main story

Look through your notes and pick the one memory that best shows who he is. The right story usually ticks these boxes:

  • It shows a real trait: loyal, stubborn, generous, chaotic but kind.
  • Guests who were not there can follow it with one sentence of background.
  • He would laugh at it too, in front of his gran.
  • It does not involve exes, illegal acts or anything his partner would hate to hear.

You might use one or two shorter memories as well, but one strong story is the backbone.

Step 4: build the structure

  1. Opening. One line to get attention, then your name and how you know him. Our opening lines guide has examples.
  2. The story. Set the scene, tell it in a few beats, and land the funny or touching part.
  3. The point. One or two lines on what the story says about him. This is what turns an anecdote into a speech.
  4. The couple. Turn to his partner by name. Say what you have seen change, or what makes them good together.
  5. The toast. Ask everyone to stand or raise a glass, and toast the couple by name. See how to give a best man toast.

Step 5: write the full draft

Write it out in full, in your own voice, even if you plan to speak from cards. Writing in full forces you to work out the order and the exact wording of the important lines: the opening, the punchline of the story and the toast.

Tips for the draft:

  • Use short sentences. They are easier to say and easier for the room to follow.
  • Give each story one clear moment, then move on.
  • Read every line out loud. If it sounds like an essay, rewrite it the way you would say it.

Step 6: cut it to time

Most first drafts are too long. Aim for three to five minutes, roughly 400 to 650 words. Cut extra stories, long thank you lists and anything that needs a lot of explaining. Our guide on how long a best man speech should be has a word count table and a cutting order.

Step 7: check the humour

Go through every joke and ask: would the groom, his partner and both families be comfortable hearing this? If not, cut it or soften it. Our guide to safe best man speech jokes has a quick test.

It also helps to read the draft to one person you trust who knows the families, ideally not the groom. They will spot anything that might cause trouble.

Step 8: turn it into cue cards

Reading a full script word for word keeps your eyes on the paper. Instead, write the key lines on a few large cards:

  • Your opening line, in full.
  • Short prompts for each story beat, such as "shed / Lego / three walls".
  • Any line where the exact wording matters, such as the punchline.
  • The toast, in full, including both names.

Number the cards and use large, clear writing so you can glance down and find your place.

Step 9: rehearse out loud

  • Practise standing up, holding the cards, at least five or six times over a couple of weeks.
  • Time it on your phone. Add about 30 seconds for laughs and pauses.
  • Practise the pauses: after a punchline, and before the toast.
  • Do one run in front of a friend, so the first time you see faces is not the wedding.

Step 10: on the day

  • Keep the cards in an inside pocket and check they are still there before the meal.
  • Go easy on the drinks before you speak. Have one if it helps, then switch to water.
  • Check where the microphone is and how it works.
  • Look at the couple during the sincere part, and at the room during the jokes.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start writing my best man speech?

As soon as you are asked, start collecting stories. Aim for a full draft about a month before the wedding so you have time to cut it and rehearse.

What if I can't think of any stories?

Ask other people. His family, old friends and colleagues will have memories, and their stories can be told in your words with credit to them.

Should I memorise my best man speech?

You do not need to. Know the opening and the toast well, and use short prompts on cue cards for the middle.

Can I use AI to write my speech?

It can help you tidy wording or suggest structure, but it cannot know your stories. The speech has to be built from your own memories to sound like you.

What should I avoid in a best man speech?

Exes, sex, embarrassing stories about family members, heavy drinking stories, and jokes only the stag group will get.
