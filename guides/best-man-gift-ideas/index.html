Best man gift ideas for the groom

The groomsmen gift and the thank-you gift are two different jobs. Here's both.

Updated 18 November 2026

THE SHORT ANSWER

As best man you usually organise a gift to the groom from the groomsmen or ushers, timed for the morning of the wedding or the night before. Pick something he will use, collect money early, and keep receipts. Separate from that: the couple may thank you with a small gift — that is their choice, not your invoice.

Two different gifts people mix up

Gift to the groom — often organised by the best man, paid for by the groomsmen together. Thank-you to the best man — from the couple, optional, and not something you should hint about. This page is mainly about the first. Money etiquette for suits and the stag sits in what the best man pays for.

How to organise the groomsmen gift

  1. Ask the groom quietly what he would actually use — or ask his partner if surprises are welcome.
  2. Set a per-person amount the least well-off groomsman can afford.
  3. Collect money before you order. Same discipline as a stag do budget.
  4. Buy once. Avoid six competing small gifts.
  5. Give it on the morning, at the rehearsal dinner, or the night before — not mid-ceremony.

Ideas that tend to land

UK gift retailers that rank for “best man gifts” lean hard on engraved cufflinks, hip flasks, whisky glasses and wallets. Use those lists as inspiration, not as a shopping order. Prefer something matched to him:

  • Useful on the day: cufflinks, tie bar, leather card holder he will carry.
  • Useful after: a decent bottle he would not buy himself, a watch strap, a photo book of the friendship.
  • Experience: a meal, tickets, or a shared activity after the honeymoon dust settles.
  • Sentimental without cringe: a letter from the groomsmen plus one solid object.

Published UK gift round-ups (for example Not On The High Street and specialist engraving shops) show many personalised options from roughly the low tens of pounds upward; treat listed prices as examples to re-check, not quotes.

What to avoid

  • Joke gifts that embarrass him in front of parents.
  • Anything that needs explaining with a stag-do story.
  • Matching “best man” novelty that only works in a photo.
  • Last-minute petrol-station bottles with no thought behind them.

If the couple thank you

A card is enough. If they give cufflinks or a bottle, say thank you and move on. You do not owe a matching spend. Your job remains the speech, the rings if asked, and keeping the day steady — see best man's job and the duties checklist.

Budget

There is no official “correct” spend. Public UK gift shops list personalised cufflinks, flasks and glasses across wide price points; US round-ups often talk in tens to low hundreds of dollars for groomsmen gifts. Use those listings only as a sense of what exists, then set your number from the people paying.

  • Low: a strong card from everyone plus one shared bottle or experience contribution.
  • Mid: one engraved or useful object bought once for the groom.
  • Higher: a watch accessory, a serious bottle, or an experience — only if nobody is stretching.

Write the per-person ask in the group chat with a deadline. Soft asks without deadlines turn into you covering the bill. Same discipline as collecting for a stag.

Timing, presentation and photos

Give the gift when he can react like a person, not a schedule. Morning getting-ready works if the photographer wants a candid. The night before works if mornings are chaotic. Avoid handing over a large box while he is trying to fix a cufflink two minutes before the car leaves.

Include a short card signed by the group. One sentence each is enough. If someone cannot afford the shared gift, let them sign the card and contribute what they can without a public announcement.

Gifts the other direction

Couples sometimes thank the best man with cufflinks, a bottle, or a small engraved item. Say thank you properly. You do not need to match the value. If nothing arrives, that is also normal — the role is not a transaction. Your real deliverables remain the speech, the rings if asked, and a steady wedding day.

If you are buying for several groomsmen from the couple’s side (rare, but it happens when you are helping the groom choose thank-yous), keep the gifts in the same band so nobody feels ranked.

Coordinating with the couple without spoiling surprises

Some grooms hate surprises. Some partners have already bought the “big” gift and do not want a duplicate watch. A quiet text to the partner — “we’re thinking of X in this budget, any veto?” — prevents wasted money. If they ask you to skip a physical gift, organise a group card and a shared experience later instead.

Keep the surprise boundary clear in the groomsmen chat. Nothing ruins a morning gift like a leaked photo the week before.

Practical examples by groom type

  • Cooks: a knife he would not buy, or a class for two after the honeymoon.
  • Travellers: a quality packing aid or a contribution to a trip fund with a written note.
  • Watch wearers: a strap or service, not another unused fashion watch unless you know the brand.
  • Homebodies: something for the flat that is not novelty tat — a framed print from a place that matters, for example.
  • New parents already: time and food often beat objects. A freezer-meal rota organised by the groomsmen can be the gift.

Frequently asked questions

Does the best man buy the groom a gift?

Often the best man organises a shared gift from the groomsmen. Agree a budget and collect money before ordering.

When should we give the gift?

Morning of the wedding, the night before, or at the rehearsal dinner are common. Ask the couple what fits the day.

How much should each groomsman contribute?

Whatever the least well-off person can afford comfortably. There is no fixed figure.

Should the groom buy the best man a gift?

Many couples do; it is a thank-you, not a requirement. A sincere card is enough.

Is a joint stag gift enough?

Sometimes. If the stag already includes a present, skip a second obligatory object and write a proper card instead.
