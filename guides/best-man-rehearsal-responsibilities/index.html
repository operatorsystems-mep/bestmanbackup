DUTIES AND THE WEDDING DAY

Best man responsibilities at the wedding rehearsal

Learn where you stand, practise the ring hand-over, and get the groomsmen's call times.

Updated 18 November 2026

THE SHORT ANSWER

Show up early, learn where you stand, practise the walk and the ring hand-over, and make sure the groomsmen know the next-day call times. If you speak at the rehearsal dinner, keep it to about a minute and save the main speech for the wedding. The rehearsal is for cues, not comedy sets.

Rehearsal vs rehearsal dinner

The rehearsal is the walk-through at the ceremony venue. The rehearsal dinner is the meal afterwards — more common as a formal US event, sometimes a quieter UK meal. Toast guidance for the dinner is in rehearsal dinner toast. This page is about the walk-through and your jobs around it.

Before you arrive

  • Confirm time and address with the couple or planner.
  • Message the groomsmen the call time and dress code for rehearsal.
  • Bring a notepad or phone note for cues you must not forget.
  • Know who has the rings for the real day — practise with stand-ins if needed.

During the rehearsal

  1. Stand where you are told — usually immediately beside the groom.
  2. Walk the processional at the pace the coordinator sets (slower than feels natural).
  3. Practise the ring hand-over until it is boring. Details: best man rings.
  4. Note where you exit and whether you escort anyone.
  5. Ask about microphones, readings, and where you put a drink before the ceremony.

US guides (The Knot, Art of Manliness, planner checklists) all treat the best man as the person groomsmen watch for timing. Be the calm clock.

After the walk-through

  • Send the group a short summary: tomorrow’s call time, parking, what to bring.
  • Confirm you have cue cards and a day sheet ready — week-before checklist.
  • Eat, hydrate, sleep. Do not treat the dinner as the stag do.

If you are asked to toast at dinner

Sixty to ninety seconds. One story, both names, sit down. Do not burn the wedding speech. Full examples: rehearsal dinner toast.

Herding the groomsmen without nagging

Send one clear message the morning of rehearsal: where, when, what to wear, where to park. Do not run a thread of five updates. At the venue, be the person who knows who is missing. If someone is late, start without performing anger — the couple do not need theatre before the wedding.

Questions worth asking the officiant

  • Exact cue for the rings — a line, a look, a pause?
  • Do you step forward or stay and hand across?
  • Where do bags, phones and water live during the ceremony?
  • Is anyone else doing a reading you might block in the aisle?
  • How long will you be standing — chairs or none?

If the ceremony is in a church, confirm whether phones must be off entirely and where ushers should redirect late guests. Church of England guidance for best men emphasises knowing the ring moment in advance; the same calm applies to civil ceremonies.

Linking rehearsal to the wedding morning

Before you leave, agree the morning call time with the groom and the party. Put it in the group chat once. Confirm who has the real rings overnight. Confirm suit collection. The rehearsal’s job is to make tomorrow boring in the best way.

Common rehearsal mistakes

  • Arriving late and forcing the whole party to wait.
  • Treating the walk-through as optional because “I’ll wing it”.
  • Practising the ring hand-over once, badly, and assuming it will improve under pressure.
  • Giving a five-minute dinner toast that steals the wedding speech.
  • Failing to pass tomorrow’s call time to the one usher who was in the bathroom.

Fix each with boring competence. Rehearsals reward the best man who loves checklists.

If the couple are stressed

Keep your voice steady. Offer to take one task off them tonight — confirming cars, texting a late groomsman, holding a spare boutonnière box. Do not add opinions about flowers. The rehearsal is not your design meeting.

UK church and civil differences

Church rehearsals may include where you sit during hymns, when to stand, and how rings are placed on a book. Civil ceremonies in hotels or registry offices may be shorter and more flexible, but the ring cue is still worth rehearsing twice. If the celebrant is meeting you for the first time, introduce yourself as best man so they know who to look at.

Carry a pen. Write the cue in your own words. “After she says rings, step left, hand both” is more useful at midnight than a vague memory of a nod.

Rehearsal dinner etiquette

In the US especially, a rehearsal dinner often follows. You may be asked for a short welcome or toast. Keep it under two minutes unless told otherwise. In the UK, a meal after rehearsal is usually informal — still arrive on time, still avoid turning it into the main speech night. Save the full best man speech for the wedding.

Day-before checklist

Confirm the ring box is packed. Confirm your suit, shoes and speech cards are in one place. Confirm the groomsmen know the call time and address. Confirm you have a charger and a backup of the speech on paper. That short list prevents most rehearsal-night panic.

If anything changed during the walk-through — a new processional order, a different ring cue — rewrite your note before you sleep. Memory is unreliable after a long evening.

Frequently asked questions

Does the best man have to go to the rehearsal?

Yes if there is one. The walk-through cannot cover your cues without you.

Should I give my full speech at the rehearsal dinner?

No. Keep any dinner toast short and save the main speech for the wedding.

What should I practise at the rehearsal?

Where you stand, walking pace, and the ring hand-over.

What if there is no formal rehearsal?

Ask the couple for a five-minute walk-through on the morning or the night before. Still confirm the ring cue.

Do UK weddings always have a rehearsal dinner?

Not always. Many UK couples rehearse then eat without US-style toasts. Ask rather than assume.
