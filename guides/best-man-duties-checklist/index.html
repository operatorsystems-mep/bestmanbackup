BEST MAN DUTIES

Best man duties and jobs: a checklist for before, during and after the wedding

THE SHORT ANSWER

A best man's main duties are to support the groom, organise the stag do, coordinate the groomsmen or ushers, look after the rings, witness the register if asked, give a speech and toast, and keep the wedding day running smoothly. Most of these jobs happen in the months before the wedding, so start with a conversation with the couple.

What the best man actually does

As Hitched points out, every couple is different: some hand out lots of jobs, others want a hands-off best man. There are no rules saying a groom must have one, how many he has, or that the best man has to be a man. Best women are increasingly common. "Best man" covers very different jobs at different weddings. Some couples want a full-time organiser; others just want a friend beside the groom and a good speech. So your first duty is to find out which kind of best man you've been asked to be.

At most UK and US weddings, the core duties are:

  • Support the groom through the planning, the nerves and the day itself.
  • Organise the stag do or bachelor party, or at least lead it.
  • Coordinate the groomsmen or ushers, so outfits, timings and jobs are sorted.
  • Look after the rings until the ceremony, if the couple want you to.
  • Act as a witness to the signing of the register or marriage schedule, if asked. In England and Wales at least two witnesses must be present (GOV.UK), and the best man is a common choice.
  • Give a speech and toast at the reception.
  • Keep the day running: timings, problems, people who've wandered off.

Start with one conversation

Before you plan anything, sit down with the groom (and ideally his partner) and ask:

  1. What do you want me to own, and what's someone else's job?
  2. What kind of stag do do you want, and who should come?
  3. Is there anything sensitive I need to know about: family, guests, budget?
  4. What are you expecting me to pay for? Our guide to what the best man pays for helps with this conversation.
  5. When and where are the key fittings, rehearsals and deadlines?

Write the answers down. It'll save you dozens of messages later.

The best man duties timeline

6 months or more before

  • Have the conversation above
  • Set up a group chat for the stag do with the groom's agreed guest list
  • Poll for stag dates, then lock one in
  • Agree a stag budget privately before booking anything
  • Book the big things for the stag: travel, accommodation, main activity
  • Start a note on your phone for speech material

3–6 months before

  • Collect stag deposits and keep a simple record of who's paid
  • Help with outfit choices and fittings for the groomsmen, if that's part of your role (Hitched suggests fittings around four to six months before)
  • If the groomsmen are giving the groom a gift, agree it and collect money around two to three months before
  • Introduce yourself to the ushers and other groomsmen
  • Ask family and old friends for stories for the speech

1–3 months before

  • Run the stag do (Hitched recommends one to three months before the wedding)
  • Confirm usher duties: who's on the door, who hands out orders of service, who directs parking
  • Write a full draft of your speech (see how to write a best man speech)
  • Check the wedding-day timings with the couple or venue coordinator

The final two weeks

  • Rehearse your speech out loud, then move to cue cards
  • Confirm transport and timings for the groom and groomsmen on the morning
  • Attend the rehearsal or pre-wedding dinner, if there is one
  • Collect any final outfits or accessories
  • Prepare a small emergency kit (see below)

The day before

  • Pack your suit, the cue cards, and the rings if you're holding them
  • Check the groom has everything he needs
  • Traditionally, stay with the groom the night before. Keep it calm and go easy on the drink.

On the wedding day

Our guide to what the best man does on the wedding day runs through it hour by hour. The short version:

  • Get the groom up, fed, dressed and to the ceremony on time
  • Keep the rings safe until they're needed
  • Make sure the ushers know their jobs
  • Witness the register if asked
  • Help gather people for photos
  • Give your speech and toast
  • Keep an eye on timings and step in on small problems
  • Help at the end: gifts, cards, the groom's belongings, getting people home

After the wedding

  • Return any hired outfits on time, if that's been delegated to you
  • Make sure gifts and cards reach the couple safely
  • Send the couple any photos or videos you collected

The best man's emergency kit

A small bag in your jacket or car can rescue a lot of moments:

  • Spare phone charger and power bank
  • Plasters, painkillers and indigestion tablets
  • Safety pins, a needle and thread, a stain remover pen
  • Tissues and mints
  • A copy of the day's timings and key phone numbers
  • Some cash for taxis or tips
  • Your cue cards, plus a photo of them on your phone

Best man vs groomsmen vs ushers

RoleTypical jobs
Best manLeads the groom's side: stag do, rings, speech, coordinating the others
GroomsmenSupport the groom, help with the stag do and the day, may also act as ushers
UshersGreet guests, hand out orders of service, show people to seats, help with parking and photos

In the UK, "ushers" is the traditional term; in the US, "groomsmen" is more common and they often do both jobs. Whatever the titles, a short message on the morning confirming who does what makes a real difference.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming instead of asking. Different couples want very different things.
  • Leaving the stag do too late. Popular dates and venues go months ahead.
  • Writing the speech the night before. It shows.
  • Being the drunkest person at the wedding. Your duties run until the end of the evening.
  • Forgetting the partner. Check in with them occasionally too. You're on the couple's team, not just the groom's.

If the job feels like a lot

It is a lot, but it's spread over months. Break it into the timeline above, delegate stag jobs to groomsmen, and remember that the groom chose you because he trusts you, not because he expects perfection. Frequently asked questions

What are the main duties of a best man?

Supporting the groom, organising the stag do, coordinating groomsmen or ushers, looking after the rings, witnessing the register if asked, giving a speech and toast, and helping the day run smoothly.

What are the best man's jobs on the wedding day?

Getting the groom ready and to the ceremony on time, keeping the rings safe, making sure the ushers know their jobs, witnessing the register if asked, helping with photos, giving the speech and toast, and quietly solving small problems until the end of the night. See what the best man does on the wedding day for the hour-by-hour version.

When should the best man start planning?

As soon as you're asked. Have a conversation with the couple first, then start the stag do planning around six months or more before the wedding, as dates and venues book up early.

Does the best man have to organise the stag do?

Traditionally yes, and it's still usual, but some grooms plan their own or share it out. Ask the groom what he'd like.

Does the best man hold the rings?

Often, yes. Some couples give the rings to someone else or keep them themselves, so check who's responsible.

What does the best man do after the wedding?

Typically returning hired outfits if that's been agreed, making sure gifts and cards reach the couple, and sending them any photos or videos.

What exactly does the best man do at a wedding?

Before the day: the stag do, outfits and supporting the groom. On the day: getting the groom ready and there on time, the rings, organising ushers and photos, often witnessing the signing, the speech and toast, and helping things run. Afterwards: gifts, cards and hired suits. Ask the couple which of these they want from you.

Does the best man have to be a man?

No. Best women and other titles are increasingly common, and the duties are the same whoever does them.
