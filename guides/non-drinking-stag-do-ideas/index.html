NON-DRINKING STAG DO

Non-drinking stag do ideas: 15 plans everyone can enjoy

Updated 4 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

There are 15 non-drinking stag do ideas on this page, for a day, an evening or a weekend. Each one is an activity the groom would enjoy anyway, a proper meal, and a time you stop, including mixed groups where some people drink and some do not.

Why groups go dry, or mixed

People skip alcohol on a stag do for ordinary reasons. The groom does not drink, or he is taking a break, or someone in the group cannot drink for health, faith, pregnancy, medication or the simple fact that they hate hangovers. Sometimes the budget is the reason. Sometimes the groom has said, plainly, that he does not want a bar crawl. None of these needs a speech from you about self-improvement. Your job is to plan a day he is glad he had.

Hitched suggests making a daytime activity the centrepiece so the weekend is not built around drinking. That is the whole trick. If the memorable part happens at 2pm, the evening can be a meal rather than a test of stamina. Games still matter. The ones in our stag do games guide work with a soft drink, and the non-drinking section on that page is the short version of this one.

In the US the same day is a bachelor party, and "dry" or "alcohol-free" is a normal way to describe it. The ideas below travel. Swap the pub lunch for a diner if that is the local equivalent, and keep the shape.

Daytime ideas

Six plans that finish in daylight, or at least start there. Each one can be the whole stag do.

  1. A walk with a proper lunch. Coast path, hill, canal, park he already likes. Book a table at the end, not a vague "we'll find somewhere". People who do not know each other talk more easily on a path than in a circle of chairs. Keep the distance honest for the least keen walker.
  2. Something competitive he actually rates. Go-karting, clay shooting, a driving experience, a climbing wall, an axe-throwing lane if that is his taste and not just a brochure. One session, then food. Do not stack three activities unless the group asked for a sports day.
  3. Five-a-side, rounders or a friendly match. Hire a pitch, mix the teams so school friends are not all on one side, and stop while everyone can still laugh. A dad or an uncle can referee from a chair. Nobody has to be good.
  4. Cook the meal yourselves. A hired kitchen, a class, or someone's house if it is genuinely big enough. Give the groom a job that is visible and hard to ruin, such as the toast or the playlist. Eating what you made is the evening sorted.
  5. An escape room, then lunch. Two rooms if the group is large, so you are not twelve people in a corridor. It forces new people to talk to each other. Debrief at the table, not in the car park.
  6. A late breakfast and one thing he loves. A match, a gallery, a market, a record shop, a car show. The luxury is unhurried time, not a wristband. This is the right plan when he has said he wants "nothing daft".

Evening ideas

An evening-only stag is a kindness to people who cannot give up a weekend. It also suits a groom who wants to be home at a civilised hour. Five ways to make it feel like an occasion rather than "just dinner".

  1. A private dining room and a groom quiz. The quiz is in the games guide. A private room means you can be noisy without performing for a bar. Order a menu where every person can eat, and put soft drinks on it with the same care as wine.
  2. A show. Comedy, if you trust the bill not to be cruel, or live music he already listens to. Sit together. Eat before or after. Do not try to add a crawl "on the way".
  3. Bowling, pool or darts as a tournament. Keep a simple score, give him a silly trophy, and book enough lanes that nobody spends the night watching. This is easy for mixed ages.
  4. The film he quotes too often. A cinema hire, where you can get one, or a living room done properly: seats, the good snacks, phones away for the length of the film. It sounds small until you do it with care.
  5. Stag awards at the house or a hired room. One hour, a handful of awards, a short toast from you, then people leave or stay as they like. The awards finale in the games guide gives you the shape. End on him, not on a forfeit.

Weekend ideas

A weekend is worth it when the group is spread around the country, or when the activity needs a morning. It is not automatically better than a day. Four formats that do not quietly become a bar crawl with bunk beds.

  1. A cottage or cabin, a walk, and one meal you cooked. The house is the venue. You are not looking for a nightlife strip. Agree quiet hours so the early sleepers are not the punchline.
  2. A city break with one booked activity and one dinner. Pick the city for the activity, not for the clubs. Share the plan in advance, including how everyone gets home at the end. A free afternoon is a feature, not a gap you failed to fill.
  3. A match, a race or a festival he already cares about. Build the day around the thing. If alcohol is part of that crowd, say so honestly and decide whether that still counts as the stag he asked for. Have a dry base: a seat together, food that is not a token, a way to leave.
  4. A split weekend. Saturday is the activity and the meal, for everyone. Saturday night can continue for whoever wants it, with a clear off-ramp so the people going to bed are not abandoning a plan. Sunday breakfast is the real ending, and it should include the people who left early.

That is fifteen. You need one, plus a backup if the weather or the numbers change. The step-by-step for dates, guest list and bookings is in how to plan a stag do.

Mixed groups

Most "non-drinking" stags are mixed. Someone will want a beer. Someone will not drink at all. The plan fails when the second person has to narrate that choice all day, or when the first person's evening is the only evening.

  • Do not make alcohol the ticket. No game that says "drink if". No round that assumes every hand has the same glass. Our games page is built so a pass is ordinary.
  • Put the main event where everyone can be fully there. Daytime activity, or a table. If a smaller group goes on afterwards, that is an extra, not the stag.
  • Order like a host. Soft drinks, good ones, on the table at the start. People should not have to ask for the exception.
  • Watch the jokes. "You're no fun" is not banter. Shut it down once, calmly, and the group will follow. If you laugh, you have set the rule.
  • Share beds and taxis with the same care. The person not drinking is often the person everyone assumes will be fine. Ask them, do not appoint them.

Tell the group the shape in the chat before anyone books trains: here is the activity, here is dinner, here is when the official plan ends, here is what is optional after that. Surprises are for the trophy, not for the schedule.

What it tends to cost

There is no honest single price, and a blog that invents one is guessing about your town. Think in tiers, then put a number on it with the group using the stag do budget guide.

  • The light end. A day in your own city. One activity, food each person can pay for as they go, home the same night. The best man's job is booking the activity and the table, not collecting a large pot.
  • The middle. A booked activity plus a shared meal, perhaps a private room. You will want deposits, because restaurants and kart tracks do not hold a Saturday on a promise.
  • The dearer end. Travel and a night away. The beds and the trains cost what they cost whether or not anyone drinks. This is the tier where you must ask, privately, what the least well-off person can do. A dry weekend is not a budget weekend just because the bar tab is smaller.

Cover the groom's share only if the group has agreed to, the same as any other stag. How that conversation works is in what the best man pays for.

How to pitch it

Say it as a plan, not as a policy. "He wants a day of karting and a long lunch, home by evening" is attractive. "We're not drinking, just so you know" sounds like a warning. Lead with the groom's preference. Mention alcohol once, in practical language: everyone orders what they want, nothing on the day requires a drink, and the optional late bit is labelled optional.

If a friend pushes back, do not debate their social life. Repeat the groom's brief and the off-ramp. The people who only enjoy a stag do if it is a crawl are telling you something about the guest list, not about your imagination. It is all right if they come for the day and leave. It is all right if they do not come. It is not all right to redesign his day so they will.

Then run it the way you would run any other stag: a meeting point, a spare half hour between things, one person with the booking references, and a short toast that is about him. The absence of a hangover is not the memory. He is.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good non-drinking stag do?

One activity the groom would choose on an ordinary weekend, a proper meal, and a finish time. Competitive days, a walk, a cook-up and a private dinner all work. The point is that he is the centre, not that anyone is drinking.

Can a stag do work if some people drink and some do not?

Yes, if the plan is not a bar crawl. Put the main event in the daytime or around a table, let people order what they want, and do not make any game depend on alcohol.

How do you suggest a sober stag do without it sounding like a lecture?

Start from what the groom wants, then offer a plan that sounds like a good day, not a restriction. Name the activity first. Mention drinks only to say that everyone orders their own and nobody is keeping score.

What do you do in the evening if you are not going to bars?

A private room and a meal, a show, bowling, or an awards hour back at the house. Give the night an ending. A stag do that fades out at 9pm can still feel complete if you planned the ending.

Is a non-drinking stag do cheaper?

Often, but not always. A local day with one activity is usually the lighter end. A weekend with travel and a night away can cost as much as a conventional trip. Set a per-head budget before you fall for the idea.
