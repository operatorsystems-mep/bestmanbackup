Best man speech for a cousin: how to write it

Updated 17 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Open as his cousin, tell one family story strangers can follow, then fill the recent years honestly — ask his partner or close friends for one story if you only see him a few times a year. Praise the couple, toast both names, and keep unexplained relatives out of the middle.

What is different when the groom is your cousin

You have childhood material most friends do not. You may also have a gap in the middle of his adult life. Use the family history for character, then borrow one recent story so the speech does not stop at age fourteen.

  • Explain family names once, or avoid them.
  • Half the room may be from his partner’s side — write for them too.
  • Do not settle scores with aunts, uncles or sibling rivalry.

Structure

PartJob
OpenName, “I’m his cousin”, one smile
ThenOne childhood or family story with a clear point
NowOne adult story (yours or borrowed with credit)
CouplePartner by name; welcome into the family if true
ToastBoth names

Three to five minutes is enough. If you are not especially close now, prefer a tight three. See brother speeches for related family tips and best friend speeches for adult friendship texture.

Opening lines

  • “I’m Ben, Adam’s cousin, and I have known him longer than anyone here except the people on that top table.”
  • “Every summer until we were fourteen our families shared a cottage. Adam made the rota. He was nine. That is still useful context.”
  • “I’m Sara. I am Adam’s cousin, which means I have photographic evidence and instructions from his mum to behave.”

Filling the gap years

Text his partner, a flatmate or a work friend: “What’s a small story that shows who he is now?” Credit them lightly (“Emma told me…”). Two calls beat an evening of padding.

Worked example

I’m Ben, Adam’s cousin. I have known him since we were both small enough to argue about the top bunk and lose to the same uncle.

At that cottage in Pembrokeshire, Adam appointed himself in charge. There was a rota for the good bedroom. He once presented our parents with a written case for why the drive should be broken up differently. He was nine. We see each other less now — that is what happens — so I asked people who see him every week. His flatmate said he still runs the house. A colleague said he is who you call when something has gone wrong, and he never makes you feel stupid for asking.

Emma told me that when her mum was in hospital, Adam made a simple plan of who was visiting when so nobody had to think. That is the nine-year-old with the rota, grown up, and it turns out it was never bossiness. It was how he looks after people.

Adam, you grew up well. Emma, you have a good one. Please raise your glasses — to Adam and Emma.

Frequently asked questions

How do you start a best man speech for a cousin?

Say your name, that you are his cousin, and offer one shared family detail strangers can follow.

What if we are not close as adults?

Keep it to about three minutes, use one childhood story, and ask his partner or friends for one recent story.

Should I mention lots of relatives?

No. Unexplained names lose the room. One relative, clearly introduced, is enough.

Can I welcome the partner into the family?

Yes, if it feels true. One warm sentence beats a long formal welcome.

How long should it be?

Three to five minutes, or shorter if the couple asked for brief speeches.
