REHEARSAL DINNER TOAST

Rehearsal dinner toast: what to say, with short examples

Updated 4 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

A rehearsal dinner toast should run about two to three minutes: who you are, one small story, and a glass raised to the couple. In the US it is a real event the night before the wedding. In the UK the closest thing is often just a meal the night before, and you may not be asked to speak at all.

What a rehearsal dinner is

In the US, the rehearsal dinner is the meal after the ceremony rehearsal, usually the night before the wedding. The Knot describes it as the moment the families and the wedding party sit down together, give a few toasts, and settle last details before the day itself. It is smaller than the reception. People know each other, or they are about to. The tone is warmer and less performed.

Traditionally the groom's parents hosted it, because the bride's parents were associated with the wedding itself. That split is much less reliable now. The host might be either set of parents, both, or the couple. The host usually pays, and the host usually speaks first. You do not need to explain the history of the dinner in your toast. You need to know who is hosting, so you can thank them if nobody else has.

If you are in the UK

British weddings often have no rehearsal dinner under that name. You might have a night-before supper with family, a welcome drinks, or nothing but an early night. If someone asks you to "say a few words" at that meal, treat it as this kind of toast: short, personal, and not a rehearsal of tomorrow's speech. If nobody has asked, do not stand up and invent a slot. The wedding speech is the job you were given.

Where the next day's speeches sit, if you are also speaking then, is covered in wedding speech order.

Who usually speaks

The Knot's rehearsal dinner checklist puts the host first: a welcome, and a toast to the couple. After that, parents and close friends who are not giving a reception speech often say something. The couple frequently close the dinner by thanking people and sometimes giving gifts to the wedding party. The Knot also notes that the best man and maid of honour traditionally toast at the reception, and that many couples now move some of those words to the rehearsal dinner so the reception stays shorter.

So the honest answer to "does the best man speak?" is: find out which night they want you. Three common versions:

  • Reception only. You can still be at the dinner, and you can still be asked to stand for a glass, but you do not deliver the speech. A single sentence of thanks is plenty if someone pulls you up.
  • A short toast at the dinner, and the main speech tomorrow. Different stories. Same warmth. Do not premiere the reception speech to a table of twenty and then do it again for a hundred.
  • The main toast tonight, and little or nothing tomorrow. Some couples want the long, personal speech in the smaller room. If that is you, you can go a little longer than the examples below, and still stay under five minutes. Our best man toast guide is the reception version of the same job.

If several people want to speak, someone needs a list. Open mic at a dinner of fifteen can be lovely. Open mic that runs past dessert, less so. The couple, or the host, should know the order before the starter arrives.

How it differs from the wedding speech

The dinner is not a smaller reception. It is a different room, and it wants different material.

  • Save the best story for the wedding if you are speaking twice. The dinner gets the story that is tender, odd, or only funny to people who already like him. The reception gets the story that still works when half the room has never heard his name said by you.
  • You can be more particular. A nickname, a sibling, a town, a terrible flat. More of the room is already inside the joke. You still should not be unkind. His new in-laws may be meeting you tonight.
  • Thanks matter more, performance matters less. People are here because they have done something: hosted, travelled, stood in a rehearsal for an hour. Notice that.
  • Do not run the wedding from the toast. Timings, seating and who has the rings are a conversation, not a speech. If you are also the person keeping the day on track, that job is in what the best man does on the wedding day, not in this glass.

The failure mode is treating the dinner as a dry run. You will sand the edges off tomorrow's speech, and you will bore anyone who hears it twice. If you want to practise out loud, do it in the afternoon, to a mirror or to one friend, not to the table.

A four-part shape

Two to three minutes is about 260 to 390 words at a speaking pace of roughly 130 words a minute. The shape below fits that. It is the same skeleton as a short best man speech, with the story kept smaller.

  1. Who you are, in one sentence. Name, and how you know the person you are toasting. If both families are hearing you for the first time, this sentence is doing real work.
  2. One detail, not a biography. A single scene. Set it in a place. Let it show a quality: steadiness, humour, the way he looks after people.
  3. The partner, in two lines. What you have noticed, not a character study. If you hardly know them, say so and wish them well without inventing closeness.
  4. The toast. Ask for glasses, say both names, sit down. Thank the hosts in that closing if it has not been said.

Write the fourth part first. You can recover from a fuzzy story. You cannot recover from forgetting the names while everyone is holding a glass. For the reception version of that ending, see how to end a best man speech.

Three short examples

All three are fiction. Change the names and the detail, or they will sound like a script, because they are one. Each is a dinner toast, not a reception speech.

Best man

Use this if you are the best man and you are also speaking tomorrow. It spends a small story so the bigger one can wait.

I'm Chris, and I'm Matt's best man, which mostly means I'll be the one asking everyone to be in the right place tomorrow. Tonight I only want to say one thing about him. When I moved cities and knew nobody, he drove over with a folding table, because he had decided I should have people round even though I owned four plates. We ate takeaway off that table for a year. It was a ridiculous gift. It was also exactly who he is.

Elena, I don't have a long speech about you yet. I have the way he says "we" without noticing. Thank you for looking after him, and for letting this slightly scruffy friendship come too.

Please raise a glass, whatever is in it. To Matt and Elena, and to their parents for gathering us tonight. The longer stories can wait until tomorrow.

Brother

A brother at a rehearsal dinner can be quieter than he will be, or would be, in a full speech. This one does not try to cover a childhood.

I'm his brother, so I have the unfair advantage of having seen him before he was impressive. I won't use it. I will say that he rang me last month, not to talk about the wedding, but to check I had somewhere to stay and someone to sit with tonight if I knew nobody. That is the man Elena is marrying. He is thinking about the edges of the room.

Elena, welcome to the family properly, not just politely. We are glad, and we are a bit protective, and we will get better at pretending we are not.

To Matt and Elena.

A friend who is not the best man

Groomsmen sometimes toast at the dinner so the best man can keep his powder dry. If that is you, do not deliver a secret second best man speech. Be a friend for a minute.

I'm Priya. I'm not the best man, which is a relief for everyone, including him. I met Jon at work, on a project that was going badly, and he was the person who stayed late without making a performance of it. He still does that. He just does it for Anna now as well.

Anna, the office version of him is a good man. The version that talks about you is a happy one. Those are not always the same person. In his case they are.

To Jon and Anna. We'll save the roast for someone who agreed to give it.

How long to make it

Read the toast out loud once. If it is over three minutes, you have a second story hiding in it. Cut that story, even if you like it. The dinner has other speakers, and it has a morning afterwards. How long a best man speech should be puts a US reception toast at three to five minutes. A rehearsal dinner toast should sit at the short end of that, or under it.

A few practical notes for the night:

  • Ask the host when the toasts are. During dessert is usual. Standing up during the main course puts you between people and their food.
  • Stay seated if the room is seated, unless you have been asked to stand. Say "raise a glass" rather than "please stand" if you are unsure.
  • Water counts. Do not wait until you have a particular drink.
  • If you will speak again tomorrow, you can even say so, as the best man example does. It gives you permission to be brief, and it tells the room the best story is being saved on purpose.
  • Thank the people who got everyone to the dinner, especially if parents have hosted. One clause is enough.

Then stop. The point of the night is that the families can talk to each other. Your toast is a door into that, not the evening itself.

Frequently asked questions

Does the best man speak at the rehearsal dinner?

Sometimes. In the US the best man often toasts at the reception, and the rehearsal dinner is left to the hosts, parents and people who will not speak the next day. Some couples ask the best man to do the longer toast the night before. Ask which night is yours.

How long is a rehearsal dinner toast?

Two to three minutes is plenty, about 260 to 390 words. The room is smaller than a reception. One story and a toast is a full speech for this night.

What is the UK version of a rehearsal dinner?

There often is not one. The closest thing is a night-before meal with family or the wedding party. If you are asked to say a few words there, keep them shorter than the wedding speech and do not use your best story twice.

Should I tell my best story at the rehearsal dinner?

Not if you are also speaking at the wedding. Use a smaller, warmer story the night before and keep the one you most want remembered for the reception.

Do people stand for a rehearsal dinner toast?

Often they stay seated, because the dinner is informal. If you want glasses raised, say so. Any drink is fine, including water.
