THE ROLES

Best man vs groomsmen vs ushers: who does what

THE SHORT ANSWER

The best man leads the groom's side: the stag do, the rings, the speech and coordinating everyone else. Groomsmen support the groom and help with the stag do and the day. Ushers look after guests at the ceremony: greeting, orders of service, seating and parking. In the US, groomsmen usually do the ushering too.

The three roles at a glance

RoleHow manyMain jobs
Best manUsually one (sometimes two)Leads the groom's side: the stag do, the rings, the speech and coordinating everyone else
GroomsmenAny number, often matched to the bridal partySupport the groom, help with the stag do and the day, often act as ushers too
UshersOften two to four, depending on the guest countGreet guests, hand out orders of service, show people to their seats, help with parking and photos

The titles overlap more than people expect. What matters is that every job has a name next to it. The best man is usually the one who makes sure that happens.

What the best man does

The best man is the groom's right hand and the lead for his side of the wedding. Typical responsibilities:

  • Organising the stag do or bachelor party, including the budget and collecting money
  • Supporting the groom through the planning and on the morning
  • Looking after the rings until they're needed
  • Coordinating the groomsmen and ushers so everyone knows their job
  • Witnessing the register if the couple ask
  • Giving a speech and toast at the reception
  • Solving small problems quietly so the couple don't have to

Our guide to the best man's job covers each of these in more depth, and the duties checklist turns them into a list you can tick off.

What groomsmen do

Groomsmen are the groom's chosen friends and family on his side of the wedding party. Their jobs are less fixed than the best man's, and are often whatever the best man and groom hand out:

  • Helping plan and run the stag do, from booking to keeping the group together on the night
  • Getting fitted and turning up in the agreed outfit
  • Being with the groom on the morning and at the ceremony
  • Acting as ushers, especially at weddings where there are no separate ushers
  • Helping with photos, the evening and the end-of-night clear-up
  • Chipping in for a gift for the groom, if the group agrees one

What ushers do

Ushers look after the guests at the ceremony. It's a hosting job more than a party job. Common usher duties:

  • Arriving early and getting a briefing on the seating plan
  • Greeting guests at the door and handing out orders of service
  • Showing guests to their seats, with close family nearest the front
  • Helping older guests or anyone with mobility needs
  • Directing parking and answering "where are the toilets?"
  • Handing out confetti if it's allowed, and gathering people for photos afterwards
  • Checking the venue for anything left behind once the ceremony ends

UK and US differences

In the UK, "ushers" is the traditional word for the men who help on the groom's side, and many weddings have ushers but no groomsmen at all. In the US, "groomsmen" is the usual term and they normally do the ushering as well. Some American weddings also add separate ushers for a large church ceremony.

Speeches differ too. At a traditional UK wedding the best man speaks last, after the father of the bride and the groom (Debrett's). Groomsmen and ushers don't usually give speeches in either country. Whatever the local custom, the couple decide, so ask. How to split the jobs

A simple way to share the work without anyone dropping a ball:

JobUsually owned byCan be shared with
Stag do plan and budgetBest manOne or two groomsmen for bookings and money
The ringsBest manRarely shared; agree one person
Greeting guests and orders of serviceUshersGroomsmen
Seating, parking and mobility helpUshersGroomsmen
Rounding people up for photosUshers or groomsmenBest man
Speech and toastBest manA joint best man, if there are two
Gifts, cards and the groom's belongings at the endBest manA named groomsman

Write the final split in one message on the morning of the wedding: who, what and when. If there are two best men, our joint best man speech guide covers sharing the speech.

Common mix-ups to avoid

  • Two people think they have the rings. Agree one person and say it out loud.
  • Nobody is on the door. If the ushers are also groomsmen in the photos, they can't be greeting guests at the same time. Plan the overlap.
  • The stag do is left to "the group". Groups don't book things. One person needs to own it, usually the best man.
  • Ushers aren't briefed. A two-minute chat about the seating plan saves a lot of awkward shuffling.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a best man and a groomsman?

The best man leads the groom's side and usually owns the stag do, the rings and the speech. Groomsmen support the groom and help with the stag do and the day, often under the best man's direction.

What is the difference between groomsmen and ushers?

Groomsmen are part of the groom's wedding party. Ushers look after guests at the ceremony, greeting them, handing out orders of service and showing them to their seats. In the US groomsmen usually do both jobs; in the UK many weddings have ushers and no groomsmen.

Do ushers or groomsmen give a speech?

Not usually. At a traditional UK wedding the speeches are the father of the bride, the groom and the best man. The couple can change this, so ask.

How many ushers do you need?

It depends on the guest count and venue. Many weddings have two to four ushers. Larger ceremonies may need more.

Who organises the stag do, the best man or the groomsmen?

Traditionally the best man, with groomsmen helping with bookings, money and keeping the group together. Some grooms plan their own, so ask what he wants.
