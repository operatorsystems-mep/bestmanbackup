BEST MAN SPEECH FOR A BROTHER

How to write a best man speech for your brother

THE SHORT ANSWER

A best man speech for your brother works best when it uses what only a sibling has: a lifetime of stories, a view of how he grew up, and the chance to welcome his partner into the family. Pick one or two childhood stories everyone can enjoy, avoid family politics, thank or mention your parents if the couple want it, and finish by welcoming the partner on behalf of the family.

What's different when the groom is your brother

Being a brother and a best man gives you an advantage and a trap.

The advantage: you have more material than anyone in the room. You know how he was at six, at sixteen and at twenty-six. You know your parents' side of every story. And you can speak for the family in a way a friend can't.

The trap: family history comes with family baggage. Old rivalries, a parent who isn't there, a relative who'll take things the wrong way. A brother's speech can get personal quickly, for better and worse.

The aim is to use the advantage and step around the trap.

Choosing the right stories

You'll have far more stories than you can use. Shortlist them, then pick one or two using these tests:

  • Is it about him, or about the family? A story about your whole family's chaotic holidays can be lovely, but keep him at the centre.
  • Does it show who he became? The best childhood stories have a link to the man he is now. The kid who organised his toys alphabetically is now the groom with a spreadsheet for the seating plan.
  • Will your parents be comfortable? They're sitting at the top table. If a story embarrasses them, it goes.
  • Does it need a family back-story? If the room needs to know about Aunt Linda's caravan, it's an in-joke.

Story ideas that work for brothers

  • What he was like as a kid, and the trait that survived into adulthood
  • The time he looked after you, or you looked after him
  • His most dramatic childhood ambition (astronaut, footballer, professional magician)
  • How he behaved when he first brought his partner home
  • A family saying or tradition he's carried on

Stories to leave out

  • Old fights or rivalries, even if they're funny to you now
  • Anything involving a parent's divorce, illness or a relative who's not there, unless the couple have asked you to mention them and you've agreed exactly how
  • Past relationships, which family members tend to remember all too well
  • Anything that makes him look childish rather than human

A structure for a brother's speech

  1. Opening and who you are. "For those who don't know me, I'm Ben, Tom's younger brother. I've spent thirty years following him around, so today feels familiar."
  2. Childhood. One story from growing up that shows a trait.
  3. Adulthood. One more recent story that shows the same trait, often in a better light.
  4. Family. A line on what he means to the family, and if the couple want it, a mention of parents or relatives.
  5. The partner. Welcome them into the family. This is something only a family member can do well.
  6. Toast.

Welcoming the partner

This is the part that makes a brother's speech special. A friend can say "we're glad you're with him". A brother can say "welcome to the family". Make it specific:

"Jess, you've already survived a Christmas with all of us and a family holiday in a leaky caravan, so you know what you're getting into. We're so glad you said yes anyway. Welcome to the family."

If the partner already feels like a sibling, say so. It means a lot to them and their family to hear it.

Mentioning parents and family

At some weddings a brother best man is expected to say something about the family. At others the groom or parents cover it. Ask the couple. If you do mention family:

  • Keep it brief and warm. A line of thanks or pride, not a tribute.
  • Remembering someone who has died. This can be lovely, but only with the couple's agreement, and usually with a single sincere sentence before moving on. The groom may want to do it himself.
  • Be even-handed. If you thank your parents, think about step-parents and the partner's family too.

Younger brother, older brother

If you're the younger brother

You've spent your life being looked after or bossed around. Lean into that: "He taught me to ride a bike, mostly by shouting." Then turn it into thanks: you looked up to him and still do.

If you're the older brother

You've watched him grow up. Gentle teasing about his younger self works, then turn it into pride: "I used to keep an eye on him. Watching him today, I don't think he needs me to any more."

Twins

Twin jokes are expected. Use one, then move on to what makes him him.

Example passage

Growing up, Tom was the brother who always had a plan. Aged nine, he drew up a business plan to sell Mum's flowers back to the neighbours. Aged fourteen, he had a five-year plan to play for Arsenal. And aged twenty-eight, he came home one Sunday, sat Mum and Dad down and said he'd met someone he wanted us all to meet properly. For the first time, he didn't have a plan. He just knew. Jess, that's when we knew too.

Emotion: how to handle it

Brothers often find the sincere part harder than friends do. Your voice might go. That's fine, and often the moment people love most. Practise the sincere bit more times than the jokes so it becomes familiar. Take a breath, pause and carry on. Our nerves guide has more help for the day.

Length and tone

The same length guidance applies as for any best man speech: five to seven minutes at a UK wedding, about six being ideal. See how long a best man speech should be. If there's also a father of the groom or parents' speech, keep yours lighter and shorter so you don't overlap. Frequently asked questions

What do you say in a best man speech for your brother?

Introduce yourself as his brother, tell one or two stories from growing up that show who he became, say what he means to the family, welcome his partner into the family and finish with a toast.

Should a brother best man speech mention parents?

Ask the couple first. A brief, warm line about parents often works well, but the groom or parents may want to cover it themselves.

How do I make a best man speech for my brother funny?

Use gentle childhood stories with a link to who he is now, and tease affectionately. Avoid old rivalries and anything that would embarrass your parents.

How do I mention someone who has died in a brother's best man speech?

Only with the couple's agreement. Keep it to one sincere sentence, then move on. Many grooms prefer to make that mention themselves.
