WRITING THE SPEECH

Best man speech template

THE SHORT ANSWER

A best man speech template has eight parts: an opening line, who you are, any thank-yous, a first story, a second story, the turn to sincere, the couple, and the toast. At about 130 words a minute, a five to seven minute speech is roughly 650 to 900 words, so most parts get between 30 and 200 words. Fill each part with your own stories, then rewrite every line until it sounds like you talking.

How to use a template without sounding like one

A template is scaffolding, not a script. It tells you what goes where and roughly how long each part should be, so you can spend your time on the bit that matters: the stories. Guests can tell when a speech has been copied, because the lines don't sound like the person saying them.

So use the template in three passes. First, fill in every part with rough notes, however clumsy. Second, pick the strongest material and cut the rest. Third, read it out loud and rewrite any line you wouldn't say in conversation. By the end, nobody should be able to see the template underneath.

Those ranges add up to roughly 500 to 880 words. Aim for the middle and you'll land in the five to seven minutes most experts recommend. The template, part by part

Replace everything in square brackets. The lines in quotes are starting points; change the wording until it's yours.

Part 1: Opening

One line that gets attention. A gentle joke about yourself, the occasion or the speeches so far works better than a joke about the groom this early.

  • "Good afternoon, everyone. For those who don't know me, I'm [name], and I've been looking forward to this almost as much as [groom] has been dreading it."

More options are in our opening lines guide.

Part 2: Who you are

  • "I've known [groom] since [how and when you met]. Back then he was [one detail about him then], and in some ways he hasn't changed at all."

Part 3: Thank-yous

At a UK wedding the traditional order is the father of the bride, then the groom, then the best man, who closes the speeches (Debrett's). The groom usually does most of the thanking. Ask the couple whether there's anyone they'd like you to thank; if not, a single line is enough.

  • "I'd like to thank [name or names] for [what they did], and say how good the [bridesmaids / wedding party] look today."

Part 4: Story one

Choose one trait you want the room to see in him: loyal, chaotic, generous, competitive, always late. Then pick the story that shows it best. Give it a set-up, a turn and a point.

  • Set-up: "A few years ago, [groom] and I [where you were and what you were doing]."
  • Turn: "Then [what went wrong, or what he did]."
  • Point: "And that's [groom] all over: [the trait]."

Part 5: Story two

A shorter story that shows the same trait from a different angle. If story one was funny, this one can be warmer.

  • "It's not the only time. When [another situation], he [what he did]."

Part 6: The turn

  • "But here's the thing about [groom]. [The trait, put kindly, and what it says about him as a friend]."

Part 7: The couple

  • "The first time I met [partner], [what you noticed]."
  • "Since then, [one specific way he's changed, or something they do together]."
  • "[Partner], [one sincere line to them]."

Part 8: Close and toast

  • "So please raise your glasses to [name] and [name]."

A filled-in example

Here's the template filled in for an imaginary groom, Tom, and his partner, Jess. It runs to about 300 words, so a real speech would add more detail to each story.

Good afternoon, everyone. For those who don't know me, I'm Dan, and I've been looking forward to this almost as much as Tom has been dreading it.

Tom and I met on the first day of secondary school, when he turned up with the wrong timetable and spent the morning in a French lesson he wasn't meant to be in. He's never been on time since.

Before I go on, Tom has asked me to say how brilliant the bridesmaids look, and I don't need asking twice.

Three years ago, Tom offered to drive us to a friend's wedding in Yorkshire. He said we'd leave at nine. We left at eleven. We missed the ceremony, arrived during the speeches and he walked straight into the photo of the bride's family. He's in their wedding album. They've never met him.

It's not the only time. He was late to his own leaving do. He was late to the hospital when his nephew was born, though in fairness, so was the baby.

But here's the thing about Tom. He's late for everything because he never says no to anyone. He'll stop to help a neighbour, take a call from a friend who's having a bad day, or go back for the thing you forgot. He's been that friend to me more times than I can count.

The first time I met Jess, she told Tom the restaurant booking was for half seven. It was for eight. He arrived at quarter to. I knew then that she was the one. Since they've been together he's calmer, happier and, for the first time in his life, occasionally early. Jess, he's lucky to have you, and I think he knows it.

He's been late for everything for twenty years. Except meeting you.

So please raise your glasses to Tom and Jess.

Notice that every story points at the same trait, and the ending turns that trait into a compliment. That's what stops a speech feeling like a list of anecdotes. You'll find four full-length speeches in our best man speech examples.

Adapting the template

  • If he's your brother, swap the "who you are" part for a line about growing up together, and choose childhood stories the whole family will enjoy. See best man speech for a brother.
  • If there are two of you, split the parts between you and agree the handovers. See how to write a joint best man speech.
  • If you met as adults, lean on the stories you do have, and say honestly what kind of friend he's been since you met.
  • If you've been asked to keep it short, keep parts 1, 4, 7 and 8 and cut the rest down to a line each.
  • If you don't know the partner well, ask the groom and a close friend of the couple for one detail about them together, and build part 7 around it.

Turning the template into cue cards

Once the speech is written, you don't need to read it word for word. The eight parts map neatly onto a handful of cue cards: one for the opening and who you are, one for each story, one for the turn and the couple, and one for the close and toast. Write the first words of each part, the key detail of each story, and the closing lines in full.

Keep the full speech in your pocket as a backup. If you lose your place, the card for the next part gets you straight back on track. Our nerves guide has more on practising with cards.

Common mistakes with speech templates

  • Leaving the template showing. If every part starts with the suggested line, it'll sound generic. Rewrite the lines.
  • Filling every slot. Not every speech needs thank-yous or two stories. Cut what doesn't earn its place.
  • Too many traits. One trait, shown well, is more memorable than five mentioned in passing.
  • Forgetting the partner. Part 7 is the part the couple will remember. Don't squeeze it into a single line.
  • Stories that need explaining. If a story only works for the three people who were there, it doesn't belong. See what not to say in a best man speech.
  • Not timing it. Read it aloud with a timer. Most first drafts are too long.

Frequently asked questions

What is the structure of a best man speech?

Most best man speeches follow the same shape: an opening line, who you are, any thank-yous, one or two stories about the groom, a turn to something sincere, a section about the couple, and a toast.

How many words is a best man speech?

At around 130 words a minute, a five to seven minute speech is roughly 650 to 900 words. A short two to three minute speech is about 260 to 390 words.

Is it OK to use a best man speech template?

Yes. A template gives you the structure, but fill it with your own stories and rewrite the lines in your own words, so it sounds like you rather than a template.

When does the best man speak at a UK wedding?

Traditionally the father of the bride speaks first, then the groom, then the best man, who closes the speeches with a toast to the couple. Many couples now change the order, so check with them.

How many stories should a best man speech have?

One or two good stories are usually enough. Choose stories that show the same side of the groom, and make sure guests who weren't there will still follow them.
