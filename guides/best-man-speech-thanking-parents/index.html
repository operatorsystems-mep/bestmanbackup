Best man speech: thanking the parents and hosts

Updated 18 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Keep thanks short, accurate and early. Name the people who hosted or paid if the couple want that said, thank both families without a roll-call, then get back to the groom and the toast. One honest sentence beats a paragraph of vague gratitude.

Where thanks sit in a UK best man speech

At many UK breakfasts the father of the bride (or another host) has already done the big welcome. Your job is not to repeat their speech. A tight thanks section — thirty to forty-five seconds — is enough unless the couple have asked you to cover hosts who were not mentioned.

Put thanks after your open, before your main story. Guests forgive a short admin block at the start. They do not forgive a five-minute gratitude list that delays the point of your speech: him, them, the toast.

Full structure: how to write a best man speech. Order of speakers: wedding speech order.

Who you usually thank

Confirm the list with the couple. Do not invent VIPs.

  • Parents or step-parents who hosted or helped
  • Anyone who gave a significant welcome earlier, if you are echoing rather than stealing their moment
  • The wedding party in one collective line, not twelve names
  • Guests who travelled far — one sentence, not a geography lesson

If families are blended, divorced or sensitive, get the wording from the couple in writing. Wrong titles cause more damage than a skipped thanks.

Lines you can adapt

  • “Before the stories: thank you to both sets of parents for today, and for putting up with the version of us that arrived as teenagers.”
  • “On behalf of Tom’s slightly chaotic friends — thank you to Emma’s family for the welcome. We felt it.”
  • “Thanks to everyone who travelled. We’ll try to make the next four minutes worth the trains.”
  • “To the hosts — you’ve made a complicated day look easy. The rest of us will now attempt the opposite with a microphone.”

Cut any line that sounds like a corporate awards night. If you cannot say it in the pub, do not say it at the top table.

What to skip

  • Listing every aunt, vendor and group chat admin
  • Money talk (“for paying for all this”)
  • Comparing which family did more
  • Inside family grievances dressed up as humour
  • Thanking people who preferred to stay unnamed

If you are tempted to pad thanks because you lack groom material, stop and find a story instead. Thanks are not a substitute for knowing him. See common mistakes.

US note

US reception toasts are shorter. One sentence of thanks is often enough before you move to the couple. The maid of honour or parents may already have covered hosts. Ask the couple who owns the gratitude so you do not triple-count the same people.

Tie it back to the toast

After thanks, you need a clean turn into your story: “That’s the polite bit. Here’s the true bit.” Then one trait, one story, the partner, the glass. Ending patterns sit in how to end a best man speech and best man toast.

A worked thanks block

Here is a forty-second block you can adapt. Keep the names the couple gave you.

Before I get Tom in trouble: thank you to both families for today. To Emma’s parents — for the welcome, the patience, and for pretending our group chat rehearsal stories were charming. To Tom’s parents — for raising someone who still texts back. And to everyone who travelled: we see you, and we promise the bar knows you’re here.

Then turn: “That’s the polite bit.” Go straight into your groom story. Do not add a second thanks later unless someone was genuinely missed and the couple asked you to fix it.

When someone else already said thanks

If the host speech covered parents in detail, your version should be a nod, not a rematch. “As we’ve already heard, today took a village — I’m not going to roll-call them again” is enough. Duplicate gratitude makes the room restless and steals time from the stories only you can tell.

Listen to the speeches before yours. If a name was skipped that the couple wanted said, you can add one clean sentence. If everything was covered, spend your time on the friendship.

Blended families and tricky tables

Get a written list. Include preferred titles. If two people should not be thanked in the same breath, the couple will usually say so — follow that even if it feels uneven. Your loyalty is to their brief, not to symmetry.

Never use the thanks section to referee a family dispute. The microphone will amplify anything sharp. If you are unsure, cut the line.

Practise the names

Mispronouncing a parent’s name is an avoidable wound. Say the full thanks out loud twice the night before. Put difficult names phonetically on your card. Look up when you say them.

Checklist before you stand

Confirm the name list with the couple. Confirm pronunciations. Confirm nobody already covered the same thanks. Confirm your story still fits the remaining time. Then stop rewriting.

Why thanks go wrong

They go wrong when they become the speech. Guests came to hear about him and them. Parents already know they helped. A crisp public thank-you is respect; a long list is delay. If you feel yourself writing a second host speech, cut it and return to the story that only a best man can tell.

Frequently asked questions

How long should the thanks section be?

About thirty to forty-five seconds. Longer only if the couple asked you to cover hosts who were not thanked earlier.

Do I thank both sets of parents?

Usually yes, briefly, unless the couple give you a different brief.

What if parents are divorced?

Use the names and titles the couple supply. Do not improvise family politics.

Should I thank the venue or suppliers?

Only if asked. Guests came for the couple, not the florist.

Can thanks be funny?

Lightly. One smile is fine; a routine about the budget is not.
