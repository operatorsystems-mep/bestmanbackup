WEDDING SPEECH ORDER

Wedding speech order: who speaks when, and for how long

Updated 4 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

The traditional UK wedding speech order is three people: the father of the bride, then the groom, then the best man. Modern weddings often add the bride, a maid of honour or another parent. In the US, toasts are split between the rehearsal dinner and the reception, and there is no single running order. Ask the couple. Do not guess from custom.

The traditional UK order

Debrett's describes the formal order as the father of the bride, then the bridegroom, then the best man. Hitched sets out the same three, and says plainly that there is no rule that forces a couple to keep them. Both are worth reading before you assume you are last, or that you are the comic relief after two serious speeches. You might be. You might not.

Father of the bride, or whoever welcomes the room

This speech opens the formal part. Debrett's says he thanks the guests and the people who helped organise the day, talks about the bride, welcomes the groom into the family, and toasts the couple. If her father is not speaking, a mother, a stepfather, a godparent or a close family friend often takes that welcome. The job is the welcome, not the job title. As best man you do not write this speech, but you should know whether thanks are being covered here so you do not repeat a list of aunties.

The groom

He thanks the person who just spoke, thanks the guests and both families, thanks you, and talks about his partner. Traditionally he toasts the bridesmaids. Many grooms now toast their partner as well, or instead. Debrett's notes that the first time he says "my wife" usually gets a cheer. Your opening, if you are next, can answer that toast on behalf of the bridesmaids. If he did not toast them, do not invent a reply to a toast that did not happen.

The best man

You close the traditional run. Debrett's expects a witty account of the groom, a sincere word on the friendship, compliments for his partner, and a toast to the couple. You may be asked to read a few messages from people who could not come. Keep those short. Then you toast, and you may be asked to announce the cake. The words themselves are covered in how to give a best man toast and how to end a best man speech. This page is about where you stand in the queue.

Traditional and modern, side by side

Hitched notes that plenty of weddings still use the old three, and plenty do not. A bride's speech, a maid of honour, the mother of the bride, the father of the groom, or two best men all show up. At LGBTQ+ weddings there is no reason to force anyone into "bride's side" and "groom's side" roles. The useful question is who the couple want heard, and in what mood.

SlotTraditional UKA common modern order
1Father of the bride, or the person giving her away: welcome, thanks, toast to the coupleA parent or the couple's host: the same welcome, whoever it is
2Groom: thanks, his partner, toast to the bridesmaidsOne partner, then the other if both are speaking
3Best man: stories, messages, toast to the coupleMaid of honour or best woman, if they are speaking
4Usually nobodyBest man, or both best men as one speech

Hitched's suggested flow when extra people speak is father of the bride, groom, bride, maid of honour, best man. Treat that as one workable order, not a new law. What matters is that funny and heartfelt alternate a little, and that the last speech knows it is last. If you and another best man are sharing, that is still one slot. See how to split a joint best man speech so you do not accidentally deliver two.

If the couple have asked a best woman to speak in your place, or beside you, the same slot applies. The best woman speech guide covers the wording. The order does not change just because the title does.

How long each speech runs

A typical wedding speech block is the thing guests feel, not your personal minute count. Three people who each "keep it to seven minutes" have already used twenty-one minutes before applause, a late microphone and the uncle who stands up unplanned. Agree a cap with the couple before anyone falls in love with a draft.

SpeakerA sensible lengthWhat usually fills it
Welcome, often a parent4 to 6 minutesThanks, one story, the toast to the couple
Groom or either partner4 to 7 minutesThanks, the partner, the wedding party
Maid of honour or best woman3 to 5 minutesOne story and a toast
Best man, main UK speaker5 to 7 minutes, and under 10Two stories, the couple, the toast
Best man, when several people are speaking3 to 5 minutesOne story and the toast
US reception toast3 to 5 minutesA shorter version of the same shape
Whole blockAim under 30 to 40 minutes including applauseWhatever the couple will actually sit through

The minute counts for your own speech, and the word counts that match them, are in how long a best man speech should be. If the couple have asked everyone to be brief, use a short best man speech example rather than a trimmed version of a long draft you will not have time to cut on the day.

Laughs, tears and a sticky microphone all add time. Write to the bottom of the range you were given. If you were given no range, ask for one. "About five minutes" is a kinder brief than "say a few words", which people hear as three minutes or twelve depending on their nerve.

Before the meal, or after

Tradition puts the speeches at the end of the wedding breakfast, once pudding is done and there is a natural pause. Debrett's has argued for that timing for a practical reason: guests who are hungry listen badly, and speeches dropped between courses interrupt the table just as it gets going. Hitched notes the newer habit of speaking before the meal, or between courses, so the speakers can then relax and eat.

Neither is wrong. They change your job.

  • After the meal. People are fed and a bit restless. You can take the longer end of your range, but you should still know you are the person between them and the evening.
  • Before the meal. Keep it tighter. A hungry room is a kind room for about four minutes and a frank room after that.
  • Between courses. You need a clean start and a clean stop, because the kitchen is waiting. Agree who cues the serving staff.

On the day itself, the practical version of this sits in what the best man does on the wedding day. Know the time you are on, not just the slot.

Who introduces whom

Someone has to start the speeches, or the room will keep talking. That person might be a toastmaster, an MC, a venue host, or a family member who has been asked. Their job is short: a welcome back to the table, the first speaker's name, and later a hand to the next name. They are not a second best man.

Ask, in this order:

  1. Is there a toastmaster or MC?
  2. If not, who is introducing the first speaker?
  3. Does each speaker introduce the next, or does the host do every link?
  4. Are you expected to announce the cake, the first dance, or a pause for the meal?

If nobody is introducing you, your first sentence has to do it. "For those who do not know me, I'm Sam, and I'm the best man" is enough. Do not tell your life story to earn the right to the story you actually came to tell.

If you are one of two best men, decide which of you is introduced and which of you is brought in by the handover. Two separate introductions make it sound like two speeches. One introduction, then your own handovers, makes it one. The staging for that is in the joint speech guide.

The US order

American weddings do not run the UK three-speech breakfast. The Knot describes a split: some toasts at the reception, some at the rehearsal dinner, with parents, the maid of honour and the best man among the people who might speak. The same piece notes that many couples move the longer, more personal toasts to the rehearsal dinner so the reception stays shorter. The Knot's rehearsal dinner guidance says the host usually welcomes the room, other family and friends may speak, and the couple often close with thanks. The best man is not automatically on that list. Sometimes you are asked to toast the night before and keep the reception to a few lines. Sometimes it is the other way round.

A few differences that catch UK best men at US weddings:

  • You may be called a toast, not a speech, even when you are still expected to tell a story. Length lives on our how long page: three to five minutes is the usual US reception range.
  • The rehearsal dinner is a real event, not a vague "night before". If you are speaking there, save the best story for the wedding. The rehearsal dinner toast guide is the one to use for that night.
  • An MC or DJ often runs the reception timeline. Get your slot from them, not from a relative's memory of another wedding.
  • People may stay seated. Ask, then tell the room clearly if you want them to stand for the toast.

What the best man should check

Have this conversation with the couple once the other speakers are chosen, and write the answers down. It takes ten minutes and it prevents the classic problem, which is two people preparing a seven-minute closer.

  • Who is speaking, in what order, on the day?
  • Is anyone speaking at a rehearsal dinner or a night-before meal instead?
  • Who is covering thanks, so they are said once?
  • Are you reading messages, announcing the cake, or introducing anyone?
  • What is your minute cap, and what is the cap for the whole block?
  • Before food, after food, or between courses?
  • Who introduces you, and will there be a microphone?
  • Are you the last speaker? If someone might add a surprise speech, who has permission to say no?

If the answer to the last question is "we'll see on the day", that is how a thirty-minute block becomes fifty. A kind best man can be the person who protects the couple's plan, including from well-meant uncles. Agree the line in advance: "We've kept the speeches to the list, and we'll raise a glass together in a moment."

Then go and write the speech you have actually been given time for. The order is the frame. The words are still yours.

Frequently asked questions

What is the traditional wedding speech order in the UK?

Father of the bride, or whoever is standing in for that welcome, then the groom, then the best man. Debrett's and Hitched both describe that order, and both say couples can change it.

How long should wedding speeches be in total?

Plan the whole block, not just your own minutes. Three speakers at five to seven minutes each, plus applause, is already about twenty-five minutes. Past forty, the room drifts. Ask the couple for a cap.

Does the best man always speak last?

At a traditional UK wedding, yes. If the bride, a maid of honour or another parent is speaking, the couple may move you. Confirm the order out loud, not from custom.

What is the wedding speech order in the US?

There is no single order. Parents, the maid of honour and the best man often toast at the reception, and some of those toasts move to the rehearsal dinner. Ask who is speaking where, and keep a reception toast to three to five minutes.

Should speeches be before or after the meal?

After the meal is the traditional UK timing, and Debrett's argues it suits the room. Before the meal, or between courses, is increasingly common so speakers can then eat. Either works if everyone knows.

Who introduces the best man?

A toastmaster or MC if there is one, otherwise the previous speaker or the venue host. If nobody is introducing you, the couple should say so, and your first line should include your name.
