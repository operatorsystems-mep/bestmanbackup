WRITING THE SPEECH

Best man speech for a same-sex wedding

Updated 18 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Write it like any good best man speech: who you are, one or two clean stories about the person who asked you, specific praise for their partner, toast both names. Confirm titles and running order with the couple. Skip stereotypes, politics-as-comedy, and jokes that treat the wedding as a novelty.

What actually changes

The craft does not change. The logistics might. Same-sex weddings often have two grooms or two brides, sometimes two best men, or titles the couple choose themselves. Guides aimed at gay and lesbian weddings — and same-sex speech examples on toast sites — stress asking about order and language before you write.

  • Ask what they call your role and their roles.
  • Ask who speaks when — do not assume the UK father–groom–best man pattern.
  • Use the names and pronouns they use.
  • Celebrate the relationship in front of you, not a history lecture.

Speech order help: wedding speech order. Joint delivery: joint best man speech.

Three to five minutes. Warm humour is fine; stereotype humour is not. Off-limits list: what not to say.

Lines and tone

  • “I’m Alex. Jordan asked me to do this job, and I said yes before the logistics email arrived.”
  • “I’ve known Sam since uni. Tonight is about Sam and Chris — both of them, equally, which somehow still surprises distant relatives.”
  • “I’ll keep this shorter than the group chat that planned the suits.”

If you want heartfelt more than jokes, use heartfelt best man speech. If you want laughs, stress-test every line with funny best man speech rules — inclusive room, no cheap shots.

Worked example

I’m Halle. Marlowe asked me to be best man, I said yes, and we agreed not to make the title a whole bit, so that is the last you will hear about it.

We met on a night shift that should have broken anyone’s patience. Marlowe kept a notebook of what guests needed and somehow still made terrible coffee funny. That attention is who he is when nobody is scoring points.

Benedict, you are the first person I have watched keep notes on him. You knew his coffee order before I did, and I sat next to him for two years. Thank you for loving that version of him out in the open.

Please raise your glasses. To Marlowe and Benedict.

Language checklist before you draft

Send the couple three questions in one message so they can answer once:

  1. What should I call each of you in the speech?
  2. What should I call my role?
  3. Is there any framing you do not want — politics, coming-out stories, family tension?

Some couples want the day treated as ordinary joy. Some want a brief acknowledgement of how long they waited for legal recognition. Neither is your guess to make. Their answer is the tone.

When there are two of everything

Two grooms may each have a best man. Two speeches should not repeat the same origin story. Agree who covers how they met as a couple, who covers childhood, and who closes. If you are the only best man for one groom, still toast both people every time you raise a glass — never “to my mate and his husband” as an afterthought name.

If titles are “best person” or names only, follow that in your opening. Guests adapt faster than speakers who over-explain.

Humour that includes the whole room

Family guests may span generations and politics. Aim jokes at universal habits: cooking, lateness, competitiveness at board games. Avoid punchlines that require the room to take a side about anyone’s identity. If a line only works in a gay bar’s comedy night, it is probably the wrong line for a mixed wedding breakfast.

The same rules as any wedding apply to exes, sex and private conflict. Same-sex weddings are not an exception that makes those topics safer; they make care more visible.

Family dynamics you may need to navigate

Most rooms are joyful. Some include relatives who are still catching up. Your speech should not scold them or centre them. Speak to the couple. Warmth directed at them is the clearest signal. If the couple have asked you to keep things light because of tension, obey that. If they have asked you to acknowledge a supportive parent, do it with one precise sentence.

Do not out private history. Coming-out stories belong to the people who lived them. If a story needs someone’s closeted past to be funny, it is not usable.

Worked toast lines

  • “To Sam and Chris — for a life with ordinary Tuesdays that still feel like a win.”
  • “To Jordan and Alex — may the group chat stay chaotic and the home stay calm.”
  • “To May and Priya — for choosing each other in public and in the small hours.”

Linking to the rest of your prep

Once tone and titles are clear, the ordinary tools apply: structure from how to write a best man speech, length from how long it should be, and cards from cue cards. Same-sex weddings do not need a different kit — they need clearer questions up front. Start with the Speech Kit when you want prompts and printable cards without inventing a process under pressure.

Humour that fits the room

Funny still works. The filter is the same as any wedding: would both partners laugh, and would the oldest relative at the top table keep their seat? Avoid punchlines that treat a same-sex marriage as the joke. The joke should be about character, friendship and shared history — not about the fact of the marriage itself.

If your best material was written for a different kind of wedding, rewrite it. Do not force old lines into a new shape. Fresh, accurate stories beat recycled ones every time.

Frequently asked questions

Is a best man speech different at a same-sex wedding?

The writing craft is the same. Confirm titles, pronouns and speech order, and avoid treating the wedding as a punchline.

What if there are two best men?

Split one speech or give two short ones. See the joint best man speech guide and agree the plan with the couple.

Should I mention equality or politics?

Only if the couple want that tone. Most rooms want a personal toast, not a speech from the news.

How do I toast two grooms or two brides?

Say both names clearly. Practise the toast line so you do not stumble.

Can humour still work?

Yes — aim at shared habits and friendship, never at identity stereotypes.
