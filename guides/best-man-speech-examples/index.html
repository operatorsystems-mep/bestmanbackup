BEST MAN SPEECH EXAMPLES

Best man speech examples, and how to make one your own

THE SHORT ANSWER

The most useful examples show you the shape of a good speech, not lines to copy. Below are four complete example speeches in different styles (funny, heartfelt, short and for a friend you met later in life), each with notes on why it works and what to swap for your own stories. Use them to see the structure, then write your own.

How to use these examples

Every example below is fictional. The groom is "Tom", the partner is "Jess" or "Chris", and the stories are made up to show technique. Please don't read them out as written. A speech only lands when it's specific to the people in the room, and guests can tell when it isn't.

What to take from each example:

  • The order of the sections. Open, who you are, stories, the couple, toast.
  • How each story is built. A beat of set-up, a turn, a point.
  • How it moves from jokes to sincerity. This is the bit most people get wrong.
  • The length. Each example is deliberately compact, about one to three minutes spoken. A full UK speech gives each story more detail and usually lands at five to seven minutes; see how long a best man speech should be.

If you want the step-by-step process behind them, start with how to write a best man speech.

Example 1: The funny speech (with a warm finish)

Good evening, everyone. For those who don't know me, I'm Dan, Tom's best man. Tom asked me to keep this short, clean and kind. So, thank you all for coming. Cheers.

(Pause.) He didn't really say that. He said "short". The other two were Jess's idea.

I met Tom at university, in a corridor, at two in the morning, when he knocked on my door to ask if I knew how to turn off a smoke alarm. I said, "What are you cooking?" He said, "Toast." It was not toast. I still don't know what it was. But that's been our friendship ever since: Tom having a confident plan, and me standing next to him holding a tea towel.

To be fair, he's improved. These days he plans everything. When we went camping last summer, he brought a laminated packing list. Laminated. In case it rained on the list. It did rain. The list was fine. We didn't have a tent, because the tent wasn't on the list.

But here's the thing about Tom. When my dad was ill a few years ago, he drove up every other weekend without being asked. He didn't make a fuss, he didn't tell anyone. He just turned up, usually with a list, and sorted things. That's who he is.

Then he met Jess. And for the first time, I saw someone hand Tom a plan and watch him follow it without question. Jess, you're the calm to his chaos, and the only person I know who can make him late on purpose. He's happier, he's kinder, and he's finally learned what a tent is.

So please stand and raise your glasses. To Tom and Jess.

Why it works

  • The opening gets a laugh within ten seconds and immediately makes the groom's partner part of the joke.
  • Both funny stories point at one theme: Tom's confident, slightly flawed planning.
  • The turn to sincerity ("But here's the thing...") uses the same theme for something genuinely kind.
  • The partner gets a real line about her personality, not just "she looks beautiful".

Example 2: The heartfelt speech

Hello, everyone. I'm Sam, and I've had the privilege of being Tom's friend for twenty-two years, which means I've seen every haircut. I won't be showing photos. I've been warned.

I want to tell you one story about Tom. When we were fifteen, I got dropped from the school football team. I was gutted, and I didn't tell anyone. The next morning Tom turned up at my house at seven with a ball and said, "Right, we're practising." He wasn't any good either. We practised every morning for a month. Neither of us got picked. But I've never forgotten that he turned up.

That's Tom. He turns up. For his family, for his mates, for strangers he met an hour ago who need a jump start. And when Chris came along, I watched him turn up in a whole new way. Chris, you've made my oldest friend the best version of himself. He laughs more. He listens more. He even phones his mum more, which she asked me to mention.

Marriage, I'm told, is mostly turning up. Turning up when it's easy and when it isn't. If that's true, you two are going to be brilliant at it.

Please raise your glasses to Tom and Chris.

Why it works

  • One story, told properly, does more than five rushed ones.
  • The theme ("he turns up") is set up, then used to describe the marriage at the end.
  • It's gentle with the humour but still gets a smile early on, which relaxes the room.

Example 3: The short speech (about one minute)

Evening, everyone. I'm Ben, Tom's brother, and his best man, mostly because I'm the only one who knows where the bodies are buried. Relax, Mum. It's a figure of speech.

Growing up, Tom was the brother who read the instructions. I was the one who threw them away. It means he's spent thirty years fixing things I broke, and never once told Mum it was me. Well, almost never.

Jess, he's been waiting his whole life for someone who also reads the instructions. You two are a perfectly organised match, and our family is lucky to have you.

Ladies and gentlemen, to Tom and Jess.

Why it works

Short doesn't mean thin. It still has an opening laugh, one specific detail, a turn to the partner and a toast. There's more on this in our short best man speech guide, and our brother speech guide covers family stories.

Example 4: A friend you met as an adult

Not every best man has known the groom since primary school. If you met at work, at a five-a-side team or through a partner, that's a story in itself.

Hi, everyone. I'm Raj. I've only known Tom for six years, which in best man terms makes me a newcomer. Most of his schoolfriends are here, so if anything I say is wrong, they'll correct me in the bar later.

We met at work, where Tom was assigned to "show me the ropes". Within a week I'd discovered that the ropes mostly meant where the good biscuits were hidden and how to look busy on a Friday. He's a generous teacher.

What I didn't expect was that the bloke from the next desk would become one of my closest friends. He's the person I ring with good news, because he'll be more excited about it than I am.

Chris, I was there the week Tom met you. He came into work on the Monday and didn't mention the biscuits once. That's when I knew.

So please raise your glasses to Tom and Chris.

A template you can fill in

Use this as scaffolding, then rewrite every line in your own voice. For a fuller version with word counts and fill-in lines for each part, see our best man speech template.

  1. Opening: one line that gets attention. A gentle joke about yourself or the occasion works well.
  2. Who you are: "For those who don't know me, I'm [name], and I've known [groom] since [how you met]."
  3. Thank-yous: only if the couple asked you to. Usually the bridesmaids or whoever the groom just toasted.
  4. Story one: a funny story that shows a trait. Set-up, turn, point.
  5. Story two: a second example of the same trait, ideally with a twist.
  6. The turn: "But here's the thing about [groom]..." and something genuine.
  7. The couple: when they met, what changed in him, something specific about the partner.
  8. Close and toast: one sincere line, then "Please raise your glasses to [name] and [name]."

Common mistakes when using examples

  • Copying lines word for word. Someone in the room may have heard them before, and they won't sound like you.
  • Borrowing a structure that doesn't fit. If you're not naturally funny, don't force Example 1. Example 2 will serve you better.
  • Keeping the placeholder details. Swap every story for a real one, or cut it.
  • Ignoring length. A strong six-minute speech beats a padded ten-minute one, and ten minutes is the ceiling most experts give. See how long a best man speech should be.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a best man speech example word for word?

It's better not to. Examples are useful for structure and pacing, but a speech only works when the stories are real and specific to the groom. Use the shape, then replace every detail with your own.

What is a good example of a best man speech opening?

A short line that gets a smile and introduces you, for example a gentle joke about yourself or about what the groom asked you not to say, followed by how you know him.

How many stories should a best man speech have?

Two or three is usually right. Fewer stories told properly land far better than a long list of quick anecdotes.

What is a good best man speech for someone who isn't funny?

Lead with warmth rather than jokes. One well-told story that shows the groom's character, a light line or two, and a sincere section about the couple will go down well without any stand-up material.
