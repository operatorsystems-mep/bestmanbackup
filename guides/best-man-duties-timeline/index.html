Best man timeline: what to do month by month before the wedding

THE SHORT ANSWER

Have the conversation with the couple as soon as you're asked. Plan and book the stag do six months or more ahead, help with outfits four to six months out, run the stag do one to three months before, draft the speech by the last month, and spend the final week on cue cards, timings and briefing the ushers.

How to use this timeline

Most best men are asked somewhere between a year and six months before the wedding. Start at the stage you're at now and pull anything you've missed forward. If you were asked late, the short-notice version further down shows what to cut and what you can't skip.

Every couple is different, so treat this as a starting list, not a rulebook. The first job on it, a proper conversation with the couple, decides how much of the rest is yours.

12 months or more before

  • Say yes properly. Tell the groom you're in, and mean it. Ask when he'd like to talk through what he needs.
  • Have the conversation. Sit down with the groom (and ideally the couple) and agree what's yours: the stag do, the rings, the speech, the ushers, anything else. Our guide to the best man's job lists what's usually included.
  • Note the date and the venue. Block the wedding weekend and the likely stag do window in your calendar now.
  • Start a speech note on your phone. Every time you remember a story, write one line. You'll thank yourself later.

9 to 6 months before

  • Get the stag guest list from the groom. His list, not yours. Ask who must be there and who to leave out.
  • Set up the stag group chat and poll for dates. Give two or three options rather than an open question.
  • Agree a budget privately with the groom before anyone books anything. Our stag do budget guide covers how to set it and collect the money.
  • Book the big things for the stag: travel, accommodation and the main activity. Popular dates and venues go months ahead.
  • Introduce yourself to the other groomsmen and ushers if you don't know them.

6 to 3 months before

  • Help with outfits if that's part of your role. Hitched suggests suit fittings around four to six months before the wedding.
  • Collect stag deposits and keep a simple record of who has paid and who still owes.
  • Agree the groomsmen's gift for the groom, if you're giving one, and collect money for it around two to three months before.
  • Ask family and old friends for stories. A short message asking for one memory each gets better results than "any stories?"
  • Read up on the speech. Start with how to write a best man speech and how long it should be. If you want a full writing pack with cue cards, the Best Man Speech Kit on Etsy (opens in a new tab) has the guide, workbook and six large cards.

3 to 1 months before

  • Run the stag do. Hitched recommends holding it one to three months before the wedding, which leaves time to recover and avoids the final rush. Our stag do planning guide goes step by step.
  • Settle the stag money within a week of getting home: refunds, final shares, and a short message so everyone knows it's closed.
  • Write a full first draft of your speech. Not perfect, just complete. Then leave it for a few days.
  • Confirm who's doing what on the day. Who's on the door, who hands out orders of service, who helps with parking. See best man vs groomsmen vs ushers for how the jobs usually split.
  • Ask the couple for the wedding-day timings and the running order for the speeches.

The final month

  • Edit the speech and time it out loud. Cut anything you'd hesitate to say in front of a grandparent. Our list of what not to say helps.
  • Move the speech onto cue cards and rehearse from them, not from the full script.
  • Confirm the morning plan: where the groom is getting ready, transport, and what time everyone needs to be where.
  • Check final outfits and accessories are collected and fit.
  • Put together a small emergency kit: safety pins, plasters, a phone charger, tissues, mints and some cash.

The week of the wedding

  • Send one clear message to the groomsmen and ushers with the timings and each person's job.
  • Go to the rehearsal or pre-wedding dinner if there is one, and learn who the key contacts are: venue coordinator, photographer, celebrant or registrar.
  • Rehearse the speech twice more, once in front of someone you trust.
  • The day before: pack your outfit, the cue cards, and the rings if you're holding them. Traditionally you stay with the groom the night before. Keep it calm and go easy on the drink.

On the day and after

On the wedding day your job is to get the groom up, fed, dressed and to the ceremony on time, keep the rings safe, make sure the ushers know their jobs, give your speech and quietly solve small problems. Hitched suggests arriving at least 20 minutes before the ceremony. Our guide to what the best man does on the wedding day runs through it hour by hour.

  • Return any hired outfits on time, if that's been delegated to you
  • Make sure gifts and cards reach the couple safely
  • Send the couple any photos or videos you collected
  • Close out any money still owed from the stag do or the gift

If you were asked late

Asked with three months or less to go? You can still do the job well. Squeeze the timeline like this:

About 3 monthsThe conversation with the couple, the stag date and budget, outfitsA big trip abroad: a local stag day or weekend books faster

Frequently asked questions

When should the best man start planning?

As soon as you're asked. Start with a conversation with the couple about what they need from you, then begin stag do planning six months or more before the wedding, as popular dates and venues book up early.

When should the stag do be?

Usually one to three months before the wedding. That leaves time to recover and settle the money, and keeps it clear of the final weeks.

When should the best man write the speech?

Collect stories from the start, write a full first draft one to two months before, and spend the final month editing, timing it out loud and moving to cue cards.

What should the best man do the week of the wedding?

Brief the groomsmen and ushers on timings and jobs, attend any rehearsal, rehearse the speech, check outfits, and pack the cue cards, rings and an emergency kit the day before.

What if I was asked to be best man at short notice?

Prioritise the speech, the rings, the wedding-day timings and the usher jobs. Simplify the stag do to a local day or night out, or hold it after the wedding if the groom prefers.
