BEST MAN COSTS

What does the best man pay for?

THE SHORT ANSWER

The best man usually pays for his own travel and accommodation, his outfit (unless the couple are covering hire), his share of the stag do plus a share of the groom's costs, and a wedding gift. The couple usually pay for the wedding itself. Customs vary between families and countries, so have an early, honest conversation with the groom rather than assuming.

Why it's worth talking about early

Money is a common source of quiet tension in a wedding party. Nobody wants to be the one who asks, so people guess, and guesses differ. A short conversation with the groom early on, about what he expects and what the couple are covering, saves a lot of awkwardness later.

Good questions to ask:

  • Are you covering outfits for the wedding party, or are we sorting our own?
  • Is accommodation for the night of the wedding being covered or booked as a block?
  • What kind of stag do are you picturing: a night out, a weekend, abroad?
  • Is there a group gift from the groomsmen, and roughly how much should it be?

What the best man usually pays for

  • Getting there. Travel to the wedding and any accommodation, unless the couple have offered to cover it.
  • What he wears. Often his own suit or the hire of the agreed outfit. Some couples pay for the wedding party's hire, so check.
  • His share of the stag do or bachelor party. Plus, by common custom, a share of the groom's costs, split between everyone going.
  • A wedding gift. Some best men also give a personal gift to the groom. Neither has to be expensive.
  • Small on-the-day costs. Taxi money, a spare phone charger, the odd round. Keep a little cash or a card handy.
  • Pre-wedding events. Travel to fittings, the rehearsal, or an engagement party if there is one.

What the couple usually pay for

  • The ceremony, venue, food and drink at the wedding.
  • Buttonholes and any accessories they want the wedding party to match.
  • Outfit hire, if they've chosen a specific look for everyone. Many couples do, but not all.
  • A thank-you gift for the best man and other members of the wedding party.
  • Sometimes accommodation for the wedding party on the night, though this varies.

The stag do or bachelor party

This is usually the biggest cost, and the best man is often the one collecting money. A few ways to keep it fair:

  • Set a budget before booking anything. Ask the group privately what they can afford and plan to the lower end.
  • Be clear about the groom's share. Say upfront that everyone is chipping in to cover him, and roughly how much that adds.
  • Collect deposits early. It stops one person carrying all the cost for weeks.
  • Offer a lower-cost option. Let people join for one day or one evening instead of a whole weekend.
  • Keep a simple record. Who's paid what, and what's still owed. A shared note or spreadsheet is enough.
  • Avoid fronting large sums yourself. Where you can, ask people to pay suppliers directly or pay you before you book.

Destination weddings

If the wedding is abroad, costs rise quickly: flights, several nights of accommodation, perhaps time off work. The same principle applies: the wedding party usually pays its own travel and stay unless the couple say otherwise. If it's a stretch, say so early. Couples planning a destination wedding generally expect that some people will need to make choices, and would rather know.

UK and US differences

In both countries the wedding party usually covers its own travel and its share of the stag do or bachelor party, and the couple cover the wedding. In the US, the rehearsal dinner is typically hosted by the couple or a family, not the groomsmen. Who pays for suits or tuxedos varies a lot from couple to couple on both sides of the Atlantic, so it's always worth asking rather than assuming.

Gifts: what's expected

For the couple

A wedding gift from the best man is normal, but it doesn't need to be extravagant. If there's a gift list, use it. A thoughtful, personal gift or a contribution to a honeymoon fund is just as welcome.

For the groom

Some best men give the groom a small personal gift on the morning: a card, something with a shared memory, a good bottle. Entirely optional.

Group gifts

Groomsmen sometimes club together. Agree the amount early and let people opt in privately.

If money is tight

Say so early and privately. Most grooms would far rather know than find out later. Ways to keep your costs down:

  • Suggest a UK or local stag do instead of abroad, or a single day instead of a weekend.
  • Look for outfit hire deals early, or wear a suit you already own if the couple are happy.
  • Share accommodation with other guests.
  • Make, rather than buy, a personal gift: a framed photo, a letter.

Being a great best man is about turning up, keeping things running and saying something kind about him, not spending the most.

Frequently asked questions

Does the best man pay for the stag do?

The best man pays his own share, and by custom the group usually splits the groom's share between them. The best man doesn't normally pay for everyone.

Does the best man pay for his own suit?

Often yes, either his own suit or the hire of an agreed outfit. Some couples pay for the wedding party's outfits, so check before booking.

Does the best man buy a wedding gift?

Usually yes, though it doesn't need to be expensive. Some groomsmen club together for a group gift instead.

Does the groom pay for the best man's accommodation?

Not usually, but some couples cover or block-book rooms for the wedding party. Ask rather than assume.

What if I can't afford to be best man?

Tell the groom early and privately. Most grooms would rather adjust plans, such as a cheaper stag do, than have you struggle in silence.
