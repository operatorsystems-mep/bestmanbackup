RAISE A GLASS

Best man toast examples: short, funny and formal

THE SHORT ANSWER

A best man toast is the moment you ask the room to raise a glass to the couple. In the UK it usually closes a longer speech. In the US, "best man toast" often means the whole short speech. Either way: one sincere line, invite the glasses up, pause, say both names clearly. Below are short, funny and formal examples you can adapt, then the simple formula behind them.

Toast or speech: what's the difference?

The words travel differently across the Atlantic.

  • In the UK, you give a speech. The toast is the last beat, when everyone raises a glass. Debrett's still describes the best man finishing with a toast to the newlyweds.
  • In the US, people often call the whole thing the best man toast. It usually runs about three to five minutes at the reception.

This page is for the glasses-up moment and for short US-style toasts. For a full speech, start with how to write a best man speech or the short best man speech guide.

Best man toast examples: short

Use these when the brief is "keep it tight" or when you are closing a longer speech.

  • "Please raise your glasses to two people who make each other better. To Tom and Jess."
  • "I promised to keep this short. For once, I am keeping a promise. To the happy couple."
  • "Here's to the adventure ahead. To Tom and Jess."
  • "Everyone, glasses up for the two people who brought us here. To the newlyweds."
  • "Tom, Jess: you found the good thing. We're all better for watching it. To you both."

Swap the names. Keep the pause before them. The names are the point.

Best man toast examples: funny

Funny means warm. The partner is never the punchline. If a line needs the stag do to explain it, cut it.

  • "Please raise your glasses, or whatever is left in them after the last speech. To Tom and Jess."
  • "Tom has always been late for everything except this. Jess, thank you for being the appointment he finally took seriously. To Tom and Jess."
  • "I've known Tom long enough to know he cannot pack a suitcase. He packed a life with you, Jess, and somehow he brought matching socks. To the couple."
  • "May your marriage have more laughter than my speech has length. To Tom and Jess."
  • "To a lifetime of good food, shared jokes and him finally loading the dishwasher without a summit meeting. To Tom and Jess."

Test every joke on one honest friend who will be in the room. If they wince, it is out.

Best man toast examples: formal

For black-tie rooms, older relatives up front, or when the couple want classic.

  • "Ladies and gentlemen, please raise your glasses to the happy couple. To Tom and Jess."
  • "Would you all please stand and join me in a toast to the newlyweds." (Only ask them to stand if that is the custom for this wedding.)
  • "It is my honour to ask you to raise your glasses to Tom and Jess. May their life together be long, kind and full of joy. To Tom and Jess."
  • "On behalf of everyone here, we wish you every happiness. Please raise your glasses. To the bride and groom." (Only use "bride and groom" if that matches how they want to be named.)
  • "To Tom and Chris: may the love you share today be the foundation of everything that follows. Please raise your glasses."

If either person is changing their name, double-barrelling, or keeping their name, check the exact form of address the week before. Getting that wrong undoes a perfect toast.

A four-line toast formula

If you are writing from scratch:

  1. A line about the groom: "Tom has always been the friend who turns up."
  2. A line about the partner: "Jess, you have given him somewhere he would rather be than anywhere else."
  3. A wish: "Here's to a life of turning up for each other."
  4. The invite and names: "Please raise your glasses. To Tom and Jess."

Four sentences. Under thirty seconds. Works at the end of a UK speech or as a standalone US toast.

Callback version: if your speech had a theme, land on it. A best man who teased the groom's terrible sense of direction might close with: "He has got lost on every trip we have ever taken. He found his way to you, Jess, and I have never seen him more sure of where he is going. To Tom and Jess."

How to give the toast, step by step

  1. Signal you are finishing ("Before I finish..." or "So, if you would all...").
  2. Give people a beat to find a glass. Water counts.
  3. Say one sincere line in your own words.
  4. Invite the toast: "Please raise your glasses," or ask them to stand if that is the custom.
  5. Pause. It feels slow. It is right.
  6. Say both names clearly, looking at them.
  7. Drink, then sit or hand back to the toastmaster.

Practise the last three lines more than any other part of the speech. More on nerves in our best man speech nerves guide. For the lines just before the toast, see how to end a best man speech.

Toast etiquette

  • Standing: traditional for the couple's toast; many weddings stay seated. Ask, then tell the room clearly.
  • What to drink: fizz is classic; any drink is fine, including water.
  • Do the couple drink? By custom, people being toasted do not drink to themselves. Plenty of couples ignore that. Nobody minds.
  • Clinking: with the people near you is friendly. Do not attempt a banquet-table tour.
  • Name order: use the order the couple prefer, or the invitation order if you are unsure.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Asking for glasses before people have them
  • Opening the speech with the toast
  • Mumbling the names
  • Adding a joke after the names (once you toast, you are done)
  • Wrong names, titles or surnames
  • A "funny" line that embarrasses the partner
