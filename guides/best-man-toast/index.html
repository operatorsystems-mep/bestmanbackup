RAISE A GLASS

How to give a best man toast, with example toasts

THE SHORT ANSWER

A best man toast is the moment you ask guests to raise their glasses to the couple, usually at the end of your speech. Signal you're finishing, say one sincere line, invite everyone to raise a glass (and stand, if that's the custom), pause, then say both names clearly. In the US the "toast" often means the whole short speech; in the UK it's the closing line of a longer one.

Toast or speech: what's the difference?

The words get used in different ways on each side of the Atlantic.

  • In the UK, the best man gives a speech, and the toast is the final line where everyone raises a glass. The best man traditionally toasts the couple. In some traditional running orders he also responds on behalf of the bridesmaids after the groom toasts them.
  • In the US, people often call the whole thing "the best man toast". It's usually shorter than a UK speech and comes during the reception, often alongside a maid of honour toast.

Either way, the mechanics of raising the glasses are the same, and that's what this guide focuses on. For the full speech, see how to write a best man speech.

How to give a best man toast, step by step

  1. Signal. "Before I finish..." or "So, if you'd all..." gives people time to find a glass.
  2. Check glasses. If drinks haven't been topped up, wait a moment or nod to the staff. Water, juice and empty glasses all count.
  3. Say one sincere line. Something about the couple's future, in your own words.
  4. Invite the toast. "Please raise your glasses" or, if the room stands for toasts, "Would you all please stand and raise your glasses."
  5. Pause. Let people get there. This feels slow; it's right.
  6. Say the names. "To Tom and Jess." Clearly, looking at them.
  7. Drink, then sit down or hand over. If there's a toastmaster or more speeches, a quick "Back to you, [name]" keeps things moving.

Example toasts to adapt

Classic

  • "Ladies and gentlemen, please raise your glasses to the happy couple. To Tom and Jess."
  • "Would you all please stand and join me in a toast to the newlyweds, Mr and Mrs [surname]." (Only use a shared surname or title if the couple are actually using one. Check.)

Warm

  • "To two people who make each other better every day. To Tom and Chris."
  • "May the love you feel today be the smallest it ever is. To Tom and Jess."
  • "Here's to a marriage full of laughter, good food and him finally learning to load the dishwasher. To Tom and Jess."

Light

  • "Please raise your glasses, or whatever's left in them after the last speech. To Tom and Jess."
  • "I promised to keep this short, and for once I am keeping a promise. To the couple."

Short and US-style

  • "Tom, Jess: here's to the adventure. Cheers."
  • "Everyone, please raise a glass to the two people who brought us all here. To the newlyweds."

Toasting the bridesmaids

At some UK weddings the groom toasts the bridesmaids or attendants, and the best man thanks them in reply. If you're asked to do this, keep it to a few warm lines: "On behalf of the bridesmaids, thank you, Tom. They've done a brilliant job today, and I can confirm they've also kept me in line." Check the running order with the couple; many weddings skip this tradition now.

A four-line toast formula

If you're stuck, this works for almost any couple:

  1. A line about the groom: "Tom has always been the friend who turns up."
  2. A line about the partner: "Jess, you've given him somewhere he'd rather be than anywhere else."
  3. A wish: "Here's to a life full of turning up for each other."
  4. The invitation and names: "Please raise your glasses. To Tom and Jess."

Four sentences, under thirty seconds, and it works as the end of a long speech or as a standalone US-style toast.

With a callback to your story

If your speech had a running theme, finish on it. A best man who told a story about the groom's terrible sense of direction might close with: "He's got lost on every trip we've ever taken. But he found his way to you, Jess, and I've never seen him more sure of where he's going. To Tom and Jess."

For two grooms

Use both names and avoid "bride and groom" phrasing: "Please raise your glasses to Tom and Chris." Check how the couple want to be referred to, and in which order.

Toast etiquette

Do people stand?

It depends on the wedding. Traditionally guests stand for the toast to the couple, and the couple stay seated because they're being toasted. Many weddings now keep everyone seated. Ask the couple or the venue coordinator, then tell the room clearly what to do.

What do people drink?

Champagne or fizz is traditional, but anything works. Nobody should feel awkward toasting with water. If you're not drinking alcohol, toast with whatever you have; nobody will notice.

Do the couple drink?

No. When you're toasted, you don't drink to yourself. The couple usually smile, nod or raise their glasses in thanks.

Do you clink glasses?

Clinking with the people near you is common and friendly. Don't attempt to clink across a banquet table.

Whose name comes first?

Use the order the couple prefer. If you don't know, use the order on the invitation, or ask.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Asking people to raise glasses before they have them. Give them a beat.
  • Toasting too early. The toast is the end. Don't start the speech with it.
  • Mumbling the names. The names are the point. Say them slowly.
  • Tacking on a joke after the toast. Once you've said the names, you're done.
  • Getting the names, titles or surnames wrong. Check them in advance, especially if one or both are double-barrelling or keeping their names.

Toast nerves

The toast is short, so it's easy to rehearse until it's automatic. Practise the last three lines more than any other part of the speech: the sincere line, the invitation and the names. If your voice wobbles, that's fine. A slight wobble on a sincere line is often the moment people remember. Our nerves guide has more practical tips.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a best man speech and a toast?

In the UK the speech is the whole address and the toast is the closing moment where everyone raises a glass. In the US, "best man toast" often refers to the whole, usually shorter, speech.

What do you say in a best man toast?

One sincere line about the couple, then "Please raise your glasses", a pause, and both names, for example "To Tom and Jess."

Do guests stand for the best man toast?

Traditionally they do, but many weddings keep everyone seated. Ask the couple or venue, then tell the room clearly what to do.

Does the couple drink when they are toasted?

No. People being toasted don't usually drink to themselves; they smile or raise their glasses in thanks.

Can you toast with water?

Yes. Any drink is fine for a toast, including water or a soft drink.
