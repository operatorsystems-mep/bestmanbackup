STAG DO AND BACHELOR PARTY GAMES

Stag do games and bachelor party games everyone can play

THE SHORT ANSWER

The best stag do games get a mixed group laughing together and make the groom feel celebrated, without anyone being humiliated or pressured to drink. Quizzes about the groom, stag bingo, secret missions, "most likely to" and an awards finale work for almost any group, from a weekend away to a quiet night in. Every game should work with a soft drink, and anyone can pass at any time.

Before the night: a few ground rules

Stag do, bachelor party or bucks party, the job is the same: get a group of people who may not all know each other laughing together, and make the groom feel celebrated. These games are chosen for that. Agree a few rules with the group first.

  • Any drink, or none. Every game should work with a soft drink. No game should ever ask anyone to drink.
  • The groom is celebrated, not humiliated. Light teasing is fine. Anything he would hate is not.
  • Strangers are not props. Don't build games around bothering bar staff or the public.
  • Everyone can pass, every time. Anyone can sit out any game or challenge, as often as they like, with no explanation, penalty or substitute task.

Games about the groom

1. The partner quiz

Before the stag do, send the groom's partner 15 to 20 questions about him and about them as a couple. On the night, ask the groom the same questions in front of everyone and compare answers. Points for every match. Keep the questions kind: first date, his worst habit, who said "I love you" first.

2. The groom quiz

A pub-style quiz in rounds, all about the groom: the basics, his history, and a round on the couple. Split into teams. The groom can act as quizmaster, which also stops him being the target all night.

3. Two truths and a lie, groom edition

Each player writes two true facts about the groom and one made-up one. Read them out and the group votes on the lie. A good way for people from different parts of his life to share stories.

4. Guess the year

Collect old photos of the groom from family and friends beforehand. Show them one at a time, and teams guess the year. Closest guess wins the point. The haircuts do the rest.

Games that run all night

5. Stag bingo

Make bingo cards of things that tend to happen on a stag do: someone gets lost, someone orders the wrong drink, the groom tells the story about his first car. Players mark them off as they happen. Nobody is allowed to make them happen on purpose.

6. Nicknames for the night

Everyone gets a nickname at the start and uses it until the last stop. Only use nicknames people are happy with, and real names are always fine for bookings, venue staff and anything urgent. Anyone who slips loses a point for their team, and that's all.

7. Secret missions

Give each player a sealed card with a small mission: get the groom to say a certain word, start a toast, get the whole group in one photo. Missions stay within the group. Reveal and score them at the end.

8. Challenge cards

A deck of light challenges, from "do your best impression of the groom" to "tell the group how you met him". Every card is optional: anyone can play it, swap it for something they'd rather do, or pass, with no penalty and no questions asked.

Quick fillers for quiet moments

9. Most likely to

Read out a prompt, such as "most likely to miss the train home", and on three everyone points at someone. Fast, funny and needs nothing. Works brilliantly at the airport or on the train.

10. Would the groom rather

Ask "Would he rather..." questions and the group guesses before the groom answers. Good for getting people who don't know him well to learn something.

11. The photo hunt

Give teams a list of photos to take during the day: the team recreating an old photo of the groom, someone pretending to be a statue, the best view in town. Keep it to your own group, never block pavements or doorways, and anyone can sit out of a photo. Most points by the last stop wins.

The finale

12. Stag awards

End the night with awards: most dedicated to the theme, best story about the groom, most likely to be asleep first. Finish with an award for the groom himself and a short toast from the best man. It gives the night a proper ending and plenty of material for the speech.

Putting it together: a simple running order

  1. Arrival: hand out nicknames, bingo cards and secret missions. Read out the ground rules.
  2. First stop: the partner quiz while everyone is fresh.
  3. Middle of the night: the groom quiz in teams, with challenge cards in between.
  4. Quiet moments: most likely to, would the groom rather.
  5. Last stop: reveal the missions, check bingo, award the prizes and toast the groom.

Daytime or weekend away? Run the photo hunt during the day and save the quizzes for dinner. Low-key night at home? The quizzes, two truths and a lie, guess the year and the awards all work round a table.

What to prepare beforehand

  • Send the partner quiz questions at least two weeks before
  • Collect old photos from family for guess the year
  • Print bingo cards, mission cards and challenge cards
  • Write the groom quiz rounds and an answer sheet
  • Make a few simple award certificates or a list to read out
  • Put the ground rules in the group chat so nobody is surprised

Planning the stag do itself

Games are the fun part. The bigger job is getting a group to agree on a date, a place and a budget, and it usually falls to the best man: in StagWeb's industry data, 56% of stag dos were planned by the best man. A few principles:

  • Ask the groom what he wants first. Some want a big weekend, some want a curry and a pub quiz.
  • Poll for dates early. Hitched suggests getting three to five dates from the groom, letting the group vote, and locking dates in six to twelve months ahead. StagWeb reports that stag groups book about five months ahead on average, so earlier gets you better choice.
  • Agree a budget. Hitched suggests floating a narrow ballpark range rather than an open question. Plan to what the least well-off person can afford. Our guide to what the best man pays for covers the groom's share and deposits.
  • Hold it well before the wedding. Hitched recommends one to three months before, and having everything paid at least two months before the stag itself.
  • Plan for non-drinkers. Hitched's stag experts suggest making a daytime activity the centrepiece so the weekend isn't built around drinking.

Collect stories for the speech

A stag do is the best chance you'll get to gather material for the wedding speech. Ask everyone to write down one story about the groom during the night. Just remember that the stories that are funniest on the stag usually aren't the ones to tell at the wedding. Frequently asked questions

What are good stag do games?

Games about the groom work best: a partner quiz, a groom quiz in teams, two truths and a lie, guess the year from old photos, stag bingo, secret missions and an awards finale.

Are there stag do games that don't involve drinking?

Yes. All of the games in this guide work with a soft drink, and none of them ask anyone to drink.

How do you make a stag do fun for people who don't know each other?

Use team games that mix groups, like the groom quiz and two truths and a lie, so people from different parts of his life share stories.

What do you need to prepare for stag do games?

Partner quiz questions sent in advance, old photos for guess the year, printed bingo, mission and challenge cards, a quiz answer sheet and a list of awards.

How do you plan a stag do for a non-drinker?

Make a daytime activity the centrepiece, swap the bar crawl for a good meal, and use games like these that work with any drink.
