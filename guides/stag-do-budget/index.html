THE STAG DO

Stag do budget: how to set it and collect the money

THE SHORT ANSWER

Set a per-head budget before you book anything, based on what the least well-off person on the list can comfortably afford. Include travel, accommodation, activities, food and the group's share of the groom's costs, plus a small buffer. Take a deposit when people confirm, set one deadline for the balance, use one payment method, and keep a simple record of who has paid and what it's been spent on.

Start with the number, not the plan

The most common stag do money problem is planning the dream weekend first and working out the cost afterwards. By then, people have got excited, and anyone who can't afford it either drops out or quietly gets into debt.

Turn it round. Before you look at venues, agree a per-head figure with the groom, then plan the best stag do you can within it. Base the figure on the person on the list with the least spare money, not the best-paid. If a few people want to spend more, they can add an optional extra, such as a second night, without changing the price for everyone else.

What to include in the budget

A stag do costs more than the headline activity. Build the per-head figure from every part of it:

CostWhat to check
TravelTrains, flights, fuel, parking, taxis between venues, the journey home
AccommodationPrice per person, not per room; minimum stays; damage deposits
Main activityPer-person price, minimum numbers, what happens if fewer turn up
FoodGroup dinner deposits, set menus, service charges, breakfast and lunch
Drinks or kittyWhether there's a shared kitty, and how much goes in
The groom's shareHis share of everything above, divided between the rest of the group
ExtrasT-shirts, props, printed games, anything for the groom to wear
BufferA small amount for price changes, late extras and the things nobody remembered

How much does a stag do cost?

It depends far more on the format than on anything else. A local night out or a day out costs a fraction of a weekend abroad. For a sense of scale, stag operator StagWeb reported an average spend of £195 a head on UK stag dos in 2024 (£214 abroad), and The Knot's 2023 survey put the average US bachelor party at about $1,500 per attendee. StagWeb's figure covers accommodation, activities and nightlife, not transport or food, so check what any quoted price includes. Yours doesn't need to be anywhere near that. A great stag do is about the group and the groom, not the bill.

If the number you've come up with looks high, the biggest savings are usually in the format: a UK weekend instead of a flight, one night instead of two, a day activity instead of an expensive evening, or a weekend outside peak dates.

Covering the groom's share

By custom, the groom doesn't pay for his own stag do, and the group splits his share. Hitched advises making that a group decision, especially for an expensive trip abroad. Agree it before anyone pays, not at the end.

The maths is simple. Add up what the groom's place costs (his travel, bed, activity and food), then divide it by the number of other people going. For example, if his costs come to £120 and nine others are going, that's £13.33 each on top of their own share. Build that into the per-head figure from the start so nobody is surprised.

Some groups ask the groom to cover his own travel, or to pay for one round. That's fine if it's agreed openly. And if the groom insists on paying his own way, let him.

How to collect money for a stag do

  • Take a deposit when people say yes. It should cover at least their share of anything non-refundable, such as accommodation or flights.
  • Set one balance deadline. Make it a week or two before your own payments are due, so late payers don't leave you short.
  • Use one payment method. A bank transfer with their name as the reference is simplest to track.
  • Send one clear message. The amount, what it covers, the deadline and how to pay, in a single message people can find again.
  • Don't front what you can't afford to lose. If you have to pay a venue before people have paid you, only commit to what you could cover yourself.
  • Chase privately. A friendly direct message works better than naming people in the group chat.

Keep a simple record

Keep one list with every person, what they owe, what they've paid and when, and one list of every booking with its deposit, balance, due date and cancellation terms. Share a summary with the group after each payment deadline. It takes ten minutes and stops any suggestion that money has gone missing.

Kitties, rounds and extras

A shared kitty can keep things simple on the night, but agree the rules first: how much goes in, what it's for, and what happens to anything left over. If some people aren't drinking, consider a food-only kitty or let people opt out of the drinks part. Anything optional, such as a late bar or a second activity, should be paid for by the people who choose it.

Ways to keep the cost down

If the per-head figure is higher than some of the group can manage, change the plan rather than the guest list:

  • Stay in the UK. No flights, no passports and fewer people who can't make it.
  • Cut a night. One night away often captures most of the fun of two.
  • Stay together. A rented house split between the group often costs less per person than hotel rooms, and gives you a base for games and food.
  • Swap the expensive evening for a daytime activity and a good dinner, or a night in with games.
  • Avoid peak dates. School holidays, bank holidays and big sporting weekends usually cost more.
  • Cook one meal. A barbecue or a big breakfast at the house saves a restaurant bill.
  • Make your own games. A groom quiz and a few challenge cards cost nothing but time. Our stag do games guide has twelve ideas.

When someone can't afford it

Money is the most common reason people miss a stag do, and most won't say so. Make it easy for them:

  • Plan at least one part, such as the daytime activity or the dinner, that people can join on its own.
  • Show the price per part as well as the total, so people can choose what they come to.
  • Keep any conversations about money private.
  • Never make anyone feel they've let the groom down. Coming to one part is still coming.

Drop-outs and refunds

Agree up front that deposits aren't refundable unless the place can be filled or the booking can be changed. If someone drops out late, see whether anyone else can take their place, or whether the venue will reduce the numbers. If neither is possible, the person who dropped out usually covers their non-refundable share. Writing that down at the start avoids a falling-out later.

After the stag do, settle up within a week. Refund anything that wasn't spent, collect anything that's still owed, and share the final tally with the group.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a stag do cost per person?

There's no set figure. Base it on what the least well-off person can comfortably afford. For scale, StagWeb reported an average spend of £195 a head on UK stag dos in 2024, and £214 abroad.

Does the groom pay for his stag do?

Traditionally not. The group usually splits his share, but Hitched advises making that a group decision, especially for an expensive trip abroad.

How do you split the groom's share?

Add up the cost of his place, including travel, accommodation, activities and food, then divide it by the number of other people going. Build it into the per-head price from the start.

How do you collect money for a stag do?

Take a deposit when people confirm, set one deadline for the balance, use one payment method with names as the reference, and keep a simple record of who has paid.

What happens if someone drops out of the stag do?

Agree at the start that deposits aren't refundable unless the place can be filled. If nobody can take the place, the person who dropped out usually covers their non-refundable share.

Should there be a kitty on a stag do?

A kitty can make the night simpler, but agree how much goes in, what it covers and what happens to any leftover money. Let non-drinkers opt out of the drinks part.
