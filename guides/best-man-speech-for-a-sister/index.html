WRITING THE SPEECH

Best man speech when the bride is your sister

Updated 18 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

You are usually standing for the groom, but the room knows you as her brother. Open as both: how you know him, and that she is your sister. Tell one family story strangers can follow, one story that shows who he is, welcome him into the family by name, then toast both. Skip sibling score-settling and anything that upstages the couple.

Why this speech feels different

Most best man speeches centre the groom because that is whose side you stand on. When the bride is your sister, you have two loyalties in the room. Guests expect you to protect her, celebrate him, and sound like family rather than a mate from the football team.

Real pages that rank for sister wedding speeches — sibling guides on ThePerfectWedding and published brother-as-best-man accounts — converge on the same shape: introduce the dual role, one character story, a welcome to the partner, toast. Treat that as etiquette pattern, not a rulebook.

  • Name both roles once: “I’m Sam, Jess’s brother and Tom’s best man.”
  • Give him his own story. Do not make the whole speech about growing up with her.
  • Welcome him into the family in plain English.
  • Keep childhood rivalry to one light line, or cut it.

Three to five minutes is enough. If the couple want brief speeches, use the short speech shape. Full process: how to write a best man speech. If you are her sister standing as best woman for the groom, start with best woman speech instead.

Stories that work — and ones that do not

  • Works: the first time you realised he was serious about her; a kindness he showed your parents; a habit of hers he somehow finds charming.
  • Works: a childhood scene that shows who she is now, told without humiliation.
  • Fails: exes, “nobody is good enough,” threats disguised as jokes, bathroom humour from shared childhood.
  • Fails: a twenty-minute family chronology. One scene beats a timeline.

Off-limits material still applies — see what not to say. Family fights and money talk stay out.

Opening lines you can adapt

  • “I’m Sam. I’m Jess’s brother, and today I’m also Tom’s best man, which means I have been briefed by both sides and trust neither completely.”
  • “Evening. For anyone who only knows me from Jess’s childhood photos: I’ve improved. Tom, welcome — you’ve passed the only test that mattered.”
  • “I’m Priya, Jess’s sister, standing here for Tom. She asked me to be nice. He asked me to be honest. I’ll land in the middle and blame the fizz.”

A worked example

About 380 words. Swap names and the Sunday-lunch detail.

Good evening. I’m Miles. I’m Maya’s brother, and today I’m also Noah’s best man. That is a lot of titles for someone who still gets called by a childhood nickname at Christmas.

Growing up with Maya meant living with someone who organised other people’s birthdays better than her own. She made lists. She remembered who didn’t eat mushrooms. She still does. The difference is that Noah somehow finds that endearing rather than exhausting, which is either love or a very advanced coping strategy.

I got to know Noah properly on a Sunday lunch that ran three hours because he stayed to help with the washing up and then stayed to lose at cards to our dad. He did not perform. He just stayed. That is still him. He shows up, he listens, and he makes a loud family quieter in the good way.

Noah, welcome. Properly. Maya, you chose well, and the rest of us noticed before you admitted it. Please raise your glasses. To Maya and Noah — may the lists stay useful, and may you keep being each other’s favourite person in the room.

Delivery notes

Look at both of them when you turn to the couple. If emotion hits on the welcome line, pause — practise that sentence standing up. Cue cards help: cue cards guide. The Best Man Speech Kit is the first place to go if you want a workbook and printable cards for this dual-role draft.

Handling parents and in-laws in the room

When the bride is your sister, both sets of parents usually know your childhood better than your adult friendship with the groom. That is useful and dangerous. Useful because a single true line about how your family sees him will land. Dangerous because old family jokes can freeze the new in-laws.

Before you write, ask your sister one blunt question: is there anything about our family she does not want aired? Bereavements, divorces, money stress, a relative who should not be named. Then ask the groom the same about his side. Write as if both answers are binding.

  • One sentence that welcomes his parents by role, not a roll-call of names.
  • If you thank people, keep it to whoever the couple asked you to thank. Do not invent a list.
  • If a grandparent is fragile or absent, a quiet mention beats a speech about grief unless they asked for it.

Brothers who speak as best man for a sister often feel pressure to “represent the family”. You do not. You represent the couple for three to five minutes. The family already knows who you are.

If you also feel protective

Protectiveness is normal. Turning it into a joke threat is not. Lines that sound like “hurt her and we will end you” make the room polite-laugh and then look at the floor. Replace the threat with a standard: “He treats her with patience on ordinary Tuesdays, which is the only test I trust.”

If you and your sister were not always close, you can say that once, lightly, and then show what changed. Rivalry stories need a clear ending in respect. If the ending is still bitter, cut the story. The microphone is not family therapy.

For delivery, practise the welcome line looking at him, then at her. If your voice catches, that is fine — pause, breathe, continue. The heartfelt speech habits help here more than a joke file.

UK and US notes for sibling best men

At a traditional UK wedding you may still speak last after the father of the bride and the groom. If your father is also speaking, agree who covers family thanks so you do not double the same aunties. In the US, you may be asked for a shorter reception toast and a separate rehearsal dinner toast — keep the dinner short. Confirm the slot; do not guess from films.

If the couple have no father-of-the-bride speech and you are tempted to fill that role as well as best man, ask them explicitly. Two jobs in one mic slot usually means a muddled speech. Better to do one job well.

Frequently asked questions

Can the bride’s brother be best man?

Yes. Couples choose who they want. You usually stand for the groom while the room also knows you as her sibling — say both once, then focus on the couple.

How long should the speech be?

Three to five minutes is plenty. Ask the couple if they want something shorter.

Should I roast my sister?

One affectionate line is enough. A roast that needs family lore to decode will lose half the room.

How do I welcome my new brother-in-law?

Say his name, one specific thing you respect, and a plain welcome into the family. Then toast both.

What if I am the groom’s sister instead?

You may be best woman or best person for him. Use the best woman speech guide and keep the same clean rules on stories.
