FUNNY BEST MAN SPEECH

Funny best man speech: how to get laughs, with a full example

Updated 4 November 2026

About these guides: written by Best Man Backup, an independent site for best men only.

THE SHORT ANSWER

A funny best man speech gets three laughs from one true habit of the groom, then turns sincere. Specifics, a callback and a joke at your own expense will do more than a list of one-liners. Keep it to about three to five minutes, and never let the last line be a dig.

Where the laughs actually come from

Most best men who want to be funny start in the wrong place. They look for jokes. The room does not want jokes about marriage in general. It wants proof that you know this groom, told in a way his aunt can follow.

Debrett's puts it plainly: the speech should be witty and affectionate, and the stories light-hearted rather than rude. That is a writing brief, not a ban on humour. The laughs that survive a mixed room come from three places.

Specifics

A precise detail does the work a punchline is supposed to do. "He is indecisive" is a description. "He kept a spreadsheet for a toaster, with a column for emotional tone" is a picture. Guests laugh because they can see it, and because it could only be him. If you cannot name the object, the place or the sentence he actually says, you do not have the story yet. Ask his sibling or an old colleague for the version with the object still in it.

Callbacks

A callback is the same detail brought back later, slightly turned. The toaster can return when he meets his partner, and again in the toast. The second time is funnier than the first because the room is now in on it. You only need one. Two callbacks of different jokes make the speech feel like a set list. One thread makes it feel like a speech.

Self-deprecation

Tease yourself before you tease him, and about as often. It tells the room the jokes are safe. "I have been trying to get him out of a restaurant in under an hour since 2014" puts you in the story as the long-suffering friend, not the judge. If every laugh is at his expense, the partner's family hears a roast. If some of them are at yours, they hear a friendship.

A three-laugh structure

Three planned laughs are enough. More than that and you start chasing the room, which is how speeches run long. BBC Radio 4, talking to a wedding speechwriter, treats ten minutes as the outside edge. A funny speech should sit shorter than that, because laughter eats the clock. Write as if you are aiming for the bottom of your range. Our guide to how long a best man speech should be has the minutes-to-words table.

  1. Laugh one, in the first thirty seconds. A small, true observation. Not a warm-up about how nervous you are, unless the nervousness is itself the joke and it is short. The room decides quickly whether you are fun to listen to.
  2. Laugh two, inside the story. This is the main one. Set it up with the detail, land it, then say what it shows about him. The point is what makes it a speech rather than a standup bit.
  3. Laugh three, as you turn to the couple. A lighter line that uses the same detail, then stop joking. The sincere sentence comes next, with no wink.

Between the laughs, talk like a person. A speech that is only punchlines feels like a list, and lists do not make anyone cry in a good way at the end. If you want the longer shape, the best man speech template still applies: who you are, one story, the couple, the toast. Humour sits inside those parts. It does not replace them.

Read what not to say in a best man speech before you fall in love with a line. Exes, the stag do, sex, money and her family are not edgy. They are how a nice room goes quiet.

A full funny best man speech

The names are made up. Felix is the groom, Amina is his partner, George is the best man. Swap in your own habit. If your groom did not research a toaster, do not keep the toaster. The shape is the part worth keeping. Use this if you want one funny speech of about four minutes, with the sincere turn built in.

Good evening. I'm George, and I've been Felix's best man for about nine hours, which is how long it has been since he last changed his mind about the buttonholes. If you have ever waited on a decision from Felix, you already know the feeling. He does not rush. He researches. Last year he spent three weeks choosing a toaster. There was a spreadsheet. There was a column for crumb tray, and a column he titled "emotional tone". He bought the second one on the list, because the first one, he said, seemed overconfident.

That is Felix. He will not be hurried into a bad choice, and he will happily take forty minutes to tell you why the choice was good. When we went interrailing he had a plan for every city and a backup plan for the plan. In Prague the backup plan was a bakery. We went to the bakery. Then we went to the other bakery, because the first one had not been peer-reviewed. I have never been so well fed, or so late for a train. I would like the record to show that I did suggest, once, that a train might also be a kind of plan. He said we would discuss it after the strudel.

The useful thing about all that care is who it is for. If I have a mess to sort, Felix is the person who stays. He will not do it quickly, and he will want to see three options, but he will not leave. I have sat in his kitchen while he compared the merits of two almost identical ways of saying sorry to someone I'd upset. It was maddening. It was also the reason the sorry worked.

Amina, the first time I met you, he introduced you and then, twenty minutes later, tried to explain the toaster. You listened for about a minute, which was polite, and then you said, "George doesn't need the spreadsheet. He's already on our side." You were right. He laughed the way he laughs when someone has caught him being himself and decided to keep him anyway.

What I have noticed since then is that the research got quieter. Not because he cares less. Because he had already decided the big thing. He still reads a menu as if it owes him money. He still leaves the house only when the shoes have been discussed. But the decision about you was the one he never reopened. He told me once, on a wet Tuesday, that he had stopped comparing. I asked what he meant. He said, "I don't need a second option." From Felix, that is a vow.

Amina, you are patient, you are funny, and you can get him out of a restaurant in under an hour, which the rest of us have been attempting since 2014. You make him braver in public and softer at home. We are very glad it is you.

So, before he asks for five more minutes to think about it, please raise your glasses. To Felix and Amina. May the toaster last, and the marriage outlast every spreadsheet.

Notes on the example

  • One habit, three visits. Indecision is the joke. The toaster sets it up, the bakery pays it off, the vow turns it into a compliment. If you remove any one of those, the speech still stands. If you add a second unrelated gag, it starts to wobble.
  • The partner gets a line that is funny and a line that is not. Amina's toaster comment is the third laugh. The next paragraph is straight. Do not wink through the vow.
  • George is in the joke. Late for the train, attempting the restaurant exit since 2014. The groom is not the only target.
  • Nothing private. No ex, no stag, no body, no money. The teasing is about how he thinks, which is a kind thing to tease.

Testing a joke on a mixed room

A mixed room means his parents, her parents, a boss, a teenage cousin and the friend who was actually there. They do not share your references. Read the speech once to one honest person who knows him and is not him. Watch their face. A real laugh, a smile and a polite nod are three different notes. Cut the nod.

Then apply four questions to every line you are proud of:

  • Would his partner be glad this was said in front of her family?
  • Can someone who has never met him follow it without a glossary?
  • Is the joke about a habit, or about a wound?
  • If it fails, can you carry on without explaining it?

If a line needs "you had to be there", it is an in-joke. Save it for the night before, not the wedding. Our jokes guide calls this the gran test, and it is the right test even if his gran is not in the room. Someone of her generation will be.

Time it standing up. Pause for two seconds where you expect the toaster laugh and the bakery laugh. If the timed read is already at the top of your range before those pauses, cut a sentence from the middle story. Do not cut the vow or the toast.

When a joke dies

It will happen to someone in the room, and it might be you. A joke dies when you expected a laugh and got a shuffle. The worst recovery is the explanation: "No, because he really did have a spreadsheet, that's the funny part." The second worst is the apology. Both tell the room to feel awkward with you.

Have the next sentence ready, and make it ordinary. In the example, if the toaster line sinks, "That is Felix" still works as a straight description. You step off the joke and into the point. Practise that step. Our nerves guide covers what to do if you lose the thread entirely: the last card should be the sincere line and the names, so you can land even if the middle falls out.

Do not try to win the laugh back with a sharper line. That is how a gentle speech becomes a mean one in twenty seconds. One quiet joke is a moment. A second attempt is a mood.

Why the sincere turn still matters

A funny speech that never becomes serious leaves the couple with a sketch of him and nothing to keep. The turn is the point of the jokes. You have shown the habit. Now say what the habit is really about, and what his partner has done with it. In the example, the research is the joke and the loyalty is the point. "I don't need a second option" only lands because we have watched him compare everything else.

Write that turn word for word. Do not trust yourself to "be sincere when you get there". Nerves make people hide back in jokes. Put the sincere lines on their own cue card, in large type, and practise them looking up. Then finish the way our how to end a best man speech guide describes: signal, turn to the couple, one true line, both names.

If you are not a funny speaker, do not force this page. A warm speech with one light line will be better than a comic speech you do not believe. The heartfelt example on the examples page is the better model for that voice. Funny is a choice of material, not a personality test you can fail.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make a best man speech funny?

Use one specific habit of the groom, bring it back later, and aim the teasing at yourself as often as at him. Three laughs, then a sincere turn, is enough for a mixed room.

How long should a funny best man speech be?

The same as any best man speech: usually three to five minutes, or five to seven if you are the main speaker at a UK wedding. Laughs add time, so write to the shorter end of that range.

What if a best man joke does not get a laugh?

Move on. Do not explain the joke, repeat it, or apologise. A missed laugh is forgotten in a few seconds if the next line is ready.

Can a funny best man speech still be kind?

It should be. The laugh has to leave the groom looking like someone worth marrying. If a line would sting in front of his parents or his partner, cut it.

Should a funny best man speech end on a joke?

No. End on one sincere line about the couple, then the toast. A light line just before that turn is fine. The last words should be warm.
