THE WEDDING DAY

What does the best man do on the wedding day?

THE SHORT ANSWER

On the wedding day the best man gets the groom up, fed, dressed and to the ceremony on time, keeps the rings safe, makes sure the ushers know their jobs, witnesses the register if asked, helps with photos, gives the speech and toast, and quietly solves small problems until the end of the night. Get the timings from the couple in advance and carry a copy.

Before the day starts: know the plan

The best man's wedding day goes smoothly when you know the schedule better than anyone on the groom's side. A week before, ask the couple or venue coordinator for:

  • The timings: ceremony, photos, drinks, meal, speeches, first dance, carriages
  • The running order for the speeches
  • Who the key contacts are: venue coordinator, photographer, celebrant or registrar, toastmaster if there is one
  • What you're responsible for, and what's someone else's job

Put it on a single sheet or in your phone. Our best man duties checklist covers the weeks leading up to this point.

The morning

Getting the groom ready

  • Wake him up on time. Agree the time the night before. Set your own alarm earlier.
  • Make sure he eats. A proper breakfast is easy to skip with nerves. Don't let him.
  • Keep him calm. Your job is to be the steady one. Keep the mood relaxed.
  • Get him dressed. Check buttons, cufflinks, tie, buttonhole. A spare collar stay and safety pins live in your pocket.
  • Check the essentials. Rings, if you're carrying them. Any letters, gifts or vows he's bringing. His phone, wallet and keys, which may end up in your pocket.

Coordinating the groomsmen and ushers

Send one clear message in the group chat the morning of the wedding: where to be, what time, and who's doing which job. Typical usher jobs:

  • Greeting guests and handing out orders of service
  • Showing guests to seats, family nearest the front
  • Directing parking or helping guests with mobility needs
  • Handing out confetti, if it's allowed

The ceremony

Arriving

Get the groom to the venue in good time. Hitched suggests arriving at least 20 minutes before the ceremony so you can greet guests alongside him. This gives him a few calm minutes and gives you time to check the ushers are in place.

The rings

If you're holding the rings, keep them in a secure inside pocket, not loose in a trouser pocket. Check you still have them before you go in. Hand them over when the celebrant or registrar asks. Some best men keep them in a small pouch so nothing gets dropped.

Witnessing the register

In England and Wales a marriage needs at least two witnesses, who sign the marriage schedule (GOV.UK). The best man is often one of them, but the couple choose. Rules differ in Scotland, Northern Ireland and the US, where witness requirements vary by state, so check locally. Bring nothing but a steady hand; a pen is usually provided.

Leaving the ceremony

Help get guests out and towards the next location. If there's a confetti moment, the ushers or the best man often organise people into two lines.

Drinks and photos

  • Round people up for group photos. The photographer will have a list. Help find the missing uncle.
  • Look after the groom. Make sure he gets a drink and some food.
  • Keep an eye on timings. If things are slipping, a quiet word with the venue coordinator helps.

The meal and speeches

If there's no toastmaster

If the couple don't have a toastmaster or venue host, you may be asked to announce their arrival, the speeches and the cake cutting. Check in advance.

Before the speeches

  • Check your cue cards are in your pocket.
  • Go easy on the drink. You can celebrate properly once you've spoken.
  • Check where the microphone is, and who's introducing you.

Your speech

At a traditional UK wedding the best man speaks last, after the father of the bride and the groom (Debrett's). Speeches usually come after the meal, but holding them before the meal or between courses is increasingly popular (Hitched). Many couples change the order, and US receptions vary, so confirm it in advance. Our how to write a best man speech and best man toast guides cover the content. If nerves are an issue, see our nerves guide.

Reading messages

Some best men are asked to read messages from guests who couldn't attend. If you are, collect them in advance and keep it brief.

The evening

  • Help with the first dance. By tradition, the best man dances with the maid of honour or chief bridesmaid after the couple's first dance, then gets everyone else onto the floor (Hitched). Check whether the couple want this.
  • Keep an eye on people. Guests who've overdone it, kids wandering, a tense family moment. You don't have to fix everything, but you can make sure someone does.
  • Be the groom's backup. Phone, wallet, keys, a glass of water, a reminder to eat something.
  • Stay reasonably sober. You're on duty until the end.

The end of the night

  • Collect gifts and cards if that's your job, and make sure they're stored safely.
  • Gather the groom's belongings, including anything he left at the top table.
  • Help organise transport home for the couple or for guests who need a hand.
  • Check any hired items are packed to be returned.

Frequently asked questions

What does the best man do on the morning of the wedding?

Wakes the groom on time, makes sure he eats, helps him get dressed, checks he has the rings and anything else he needs, and coordinates the groomsmen and ushers.

When does the best man give his speech?

At a traditional UK wedding the best man speaks last, after the father of the bride and the groom, usually after the meal. Running orders vary, so confirm with the couple.

Does the best man sign the marriage register?

Often, yes. In England and Wales at least two witnesses must be present and sign, and the best man is a common choice, but the couple decide who.

What should the best man carry on the wedding day?

The rings if he's holding them, his cue cards, a copy of the timings and key numbers, a phone charger, safety pins, plasters and some cash.
