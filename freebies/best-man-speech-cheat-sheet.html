If you blank, you still know the next line.

The worry is standing up with nothing to say. Five parts, in this order, so you always know what comes next.

  1. Open

    Who you are, and how you know him, in one line. Thank the hosts if no one else has.

    "For those who don't know me, I'm [name]. I've known [groom] since [when]."

  2. The story

    One true moment. The scene, what happened, the detail you still laugh about, and why it matters. Then stop.

    One story is enough. Do not stack three.

  3. The groom now

    One quality you genuinely rate in him today. Just one. Not a list of old jokes.

    "What I rate in him is [one quality], and you saw it in that story."

  4. The couple

    What changed when they met. Welcome his partner warmly, by name.

    Say the name. Do not make the partner the joke.

  5. The toast

    Ask everyone to stand, raise a glass, and name the couple. Then sit down.

    "Please stand, and raise a glass to [partner] and [groom]."

650 to 900 words

That's roughly what you'll say in five to seven minutes at a calm, unhurried pace, with room for laughs. Say it out loud. If you run over, cut a story, not the toast.
