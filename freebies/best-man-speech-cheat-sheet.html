The Best Man's 1-Page
Speech Cheat Sheet

Everything that matters, on one page. Pin it up, then start writing.

01 · THE STRUCTURE

Five parts. In this order.

  1. Open. Who you are and how you know him, in one line. Thank the hosts if no one else has.
  2. The groom. Pick one quality you genuinely rate in him. Just one.
  3. The story. One true moment that shows that quality: the scene, what happened, the detail you still laugh about, and why it matters.
  4. The couple. What changed when they met. Welcome his partner warmly, by name.
  5. The toast. Ask everyone to stand, raise a glass and name the couple. Then sit down.

02 · FIVE OPENING LINES

Start strong. Adapt one.

  • "For those who don't know me, I'm [name]. I've known [groom] since [when], which means I know far too much. Don't worry, most of it stays with me."
  • "When [groom] asked me to be best man, I said yes straight away. Then he mentioned the speech."
  • "I've been asked to keep this short, so I've cut everything about [harmless habit]. That leaves about four minutes."
  • "[Groom] and I have been friends for [number] years. In that time he's taught me a lot. Mostly about [harmless thing]."
  • "Before I start, on behalf of [partner] and [groom], thank you all for being here today."

03 · THE TIMING RULE

Five to seven minutes.

650 to 900 words

That's roughly what you'll say in five to seven minutes at a calm, unhurried pace, with room for laughs.

Time yourself out loud, not in your head. If you run over, cut a story, not the toast.

04 · THINGS NEVER TO SAY

Leave these out.

  • Exes, past partners or "the one before".
  • Stag do stories that need "you had to be there", or that would embarrass anyone in the room.
  • "Ball and chain" jokes, or anything about the marriage not lasting.
  • Digs at either family, or at the cost of the wedding.
  • Secrets the couple don't know you're about to share.
  • In-jokes only three people will get.
  • "I'm not good at speeches." Skip the apology and start.
  • Anything you wouldn't say in front of his grandmother.

The test: would he laugh at it too, and would his partner be glad you said it?
